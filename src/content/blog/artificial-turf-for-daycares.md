## Start with the equipment and the children using it

List the equipment, its fall heights and the ages it serves. Mark the required use zones on the plan. A lawn appearance or a soft feel underfoot does not establish that a surface is suitable beneath a climber or swing.

Ask the supplier for impact-test documentation applying to the complete proposed system and equipment, including the pad and installation conditions. Obtain the documents for the option being proposed before ordering, and keep them with the facility’s records.

## Confirm the requirements for your program

Confirm the requirements for the state and program type. For Colorado child care centers, turf with a resilient pad is among the approved unitary materials, with manufacturer documentation requirements. South Carolina centers need cushioning under specified equipment; confirm the proposed turf system with DSS licensing. For Florida facilities, confirm that a proposed unitary surface meets the testing requirements and keep the required documents at the facility.

Send the licensing contact a layout and the actual product-system information. Ask whether additional local reviews apply and keep the response before scheduling work. A residential lawn rule should not be used as approval for a daycare playground.

## Make the operating plan part of the design

Plan shade over areas where children sit, crawl and play. Synthetic turf can become hot in direct sun, so include a procedure for checking conditions and changing play plans when needed. The [children’s turf article](/blog/is-artificial-turf-safe-for-kids/) covers that concern.

Include access for cleaning, routine waste and debris removal, and inspections of seams, edges and the area around equipment. Agree who will care for the surface and follow its instructions. Agree the closure and temporary-access arrangements in the installation schedule.

## A planning checklist for directors

- Identify the program type, equipment and fall heights.
- Obtain the licensing authority’s requirements for the proposal.
- Match test documentation to the complete installed surface.
- Mark use zones, drainage and accessible routes.
- Plan shade, condition checks and routine cleaning.
- Put installation scope and handover documents in the estimate.

For [commercial turf planning](/services/commercial-turf/), bring the equipment plan and program requirements to the free site visit. Those details help define the layout and work to be priced.
