## The short answer

Florida's gopher tortoise is a state-listed species, and its burrow is protected whether or not a tortoise is inside it at the moment you look. The [Florida Fish and Wildlife Conservation Commission](https://myfwc.com/license/wildlife/gopher-tortoise-permits/) (FWC) draws the line at 25 feet. Excavation, grading or construction within 25 feet of a burrow entrance counts as a disturbance and needs a permit. Stay outside that radius and ordinary yard work, including a turf install that doesn't touch the burrow, doesn't need one.

That distance is measured from the burrow entrance in every direction, not just toward the house. A burrow near a side or back fence can put part of a yard inside the 25-foot ring even if the burrow itself sits on the neighbor's lot.

## What counts as routine maintenance, and what doesn't

FWC draws a clear line between upkeep and construction. Its permitting page states that "most activities associated with residential lawn and landscape maintenance do not require a permit provided activities do not collapse gopher tortoise burrows or harm gopher tortoises" ([FWC Gopher Tortoise Permits](https://myfwc.com/license/wildlife/gopher-tortoise-permits/)).

Mowing, edging, hand weeding and normal irrigation repair fall on the maintenance side. A turf installation is a different scope of work. Base preparation for [artificial turf](/services/residential-turf/) typically means excavating several inches of native soil, grading it to drain and compacting a stone base — the kind of ground disturbance FWC's rule is written for, not the mowing it excuses.

FWC's own definition of a disturbance is broad on purpose: "a disturbance includes any type of work within 25 feet of a gopher tortoise burrow" ([FWC](https://myfwc.com/license/wildlife/gopher-tortoise-permits/)). It doesn't ask whether the work looks aggressive. It asks how far the work sits from the entrance.

## Measuring the 25 feet

The FAQ page states the avoidance standard plainly: "an FWC permit is not required if all gopher tortoise burrows are avoided by 25 feet in all directions during development activities" ([FWC FAQs](https://myfwc.com/wildlifehabitats/wildlife/gopher-tortoise/faqs/)).

Before any quote is signed, that means:

- Finding every burrow entrance on or near the planned turf area, including ones that are old, sanded-over or currently unoccupied. FWC treats a burrow as potentially occupied unless a survey says otherwise.
- Measuring 25 feet outward from each entrance, in every direction, not just toward the excavation.
- Checking whether the planned base, drainage grading or edging track falls inside any of those circles.

If the whole project sits outside every 25-foot ring, no permit is needed on that basis. If any part of it falls inside, the project needs FWC authorization before ground is broken there.

## The burrow doesn't have to be on your lot

This is the part homeowners miss. FWC's own FAQ warns that responsibility follows the work, not the property line: "if land clearing or other development actions on your property occur within 25 feet of the tortoise burrow on the adjacent property, you may still be held responsible if those actions result in the collapse of the tortoise burrow and/or harm the resident tortoise" ([FWC FAQs](https://myfwc.com/wildlifehabitats/wildlife/gopher-tortoise/faqs/)).

In practice: a burrow on the next lot still counts against your project if your excavation reaches within 25 feet of it. Walk the boundary line with the next yard in view, not just your own, before finalizing where the turf base will be cut.

## When a permit is needed: the 10 or Fewer Burrows Permit

For a residential lot with a small number of burrows, FWC's route for a homeowner is the 10 or Fewer Burrows Permit. According to FWC's own instructions, it asks for:

- **An e-learning course and quiz.** The applicant completes "the e-Learning curriculum and register[s] for the course," then passes the online quiz before continuing the application ([FWC, 10 or Fewer Burrows Permit](https://myfwc.com/license/wildlife/gopher-tortoise-permits/10-or-fewer-burrows/property-owners/)).
- **A site plan.** One showing "all construction/development planned for the site," so FWC can see how the work relates to the burrows.
- **An on-site recipient area.** At least 750 square feet of suitable habitat, at least 10 feet wide, with half of that area at least 25 feet from the construction boundary. FWC gives 10 feet by 75 feet as one example that meets the size and width test.
- **An aerial photo** showing the project boundary, the recipient area, any pen location and the silt fencing around construction.

This permit moves the tortoise on-site, into a protected area of the same lot, rather than trucking it elsewhere. It's built for exactly the scale of a residential yard.

## Before a quote gets signed

A burrow on or near a Florida lot changes the order of operations. The tortoise question gets answered before the turf layout gets finalized, not after.

- **Walk the whole yard, fence to fence,** looking for burrow entrances — a tortoise burrow has a distinct half-moon cross-section, wider than tall.
- **Measure 25 feet from every entrance,** including any near or on the neighbor's side.
- **Mark where the planned turf base, drainage grading and any trenching fall** relative to those circles.
- **If anything falls inside 25 feet, call the regional FWC gopher tortoise conservation biologist** for your area before signing anything. FWC's guidance points anyone in this situation to "the regional gopher tortoise conservation biologist for further direction" ([FWC FAQs](https://myfwc.com/wildlifehabitats/wildlife/gopher-tortoise/faqs/)).
- **If a permit is needed, build the e-learning, site plan and recipient-area steps into the project timeline** before excavation, not during it.
