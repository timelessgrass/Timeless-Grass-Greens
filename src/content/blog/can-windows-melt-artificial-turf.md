## Look for a pattern

Photograph the affected turf and note the time, weather and position of any bright reflection nearby. Observe from a safe position without looking into the concentrated reflection. The sun’s position changes, so a single inspection may not reveal when the area is exposed.

Include upstairs and neighboring windows in the information you give the assessor. Do not assume that a glass defect or a particular window type has been proved by the turf damage. Direct sun and other heat sources also belong in the discussion.

## Address the reflection before the patch

Replacing the turf without addressing recurring exposure can leave the replacement in the same conditions. A window professional can advise whether an exterior screen, shading or another glass-compatible option is appropriate. Check the window instructions and any warranty conditions before applying a film or making alterations.

The landscape may offer another option: change the turf footprint or place a suitable feature between the reflection and the surface. Consider how that change affects access, planting, drainage and neighboring property. A general recommendation for a screen is not confirmation that it will fit or solve your particular window’s reflection.

## Plan the repair

Give the installer the product information, installation date and photographs. Ask whether a patch is feasible and how closely the available material may match the existing turf. Include any seam or edge work in the proposal.

The [turf replacement service](/services/turf-removal-and-replacement/) can help scope the affected area. For ordinary hot-surface conditions rather than a concentrated reflection, see [turf and summer heat](/blog/how-hot-does-artificial-turf-get/).

## Before replacing damaged turf

- Record the location and timing of the damage or reflection.
- Have the likely source assessed.
- Check any window alteration with the appropriate professional.
- Confirm the changed conditions before laying replacement material.
- Agree the repair area and expectations for matching.
