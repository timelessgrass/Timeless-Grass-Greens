## Give snowmelt a route

Show the installer the paths, downspouts, drains and places where snow is usually piled. Ask how the proposed grading and base will handle those conditions. A general description of Front Range clay does not establish the soil or preparation needed at your address.

If the property has a known ground-movement or drainage issue, provide the available reports before the layout is finalized. The estimate should identify any investigation or site work included, rather than assume a standard base will resolve an existing problem.

## Clear only what you need to use

Where access is not needed, allow snow to melt naturally. Where it is needed, follow the product’s directions for approved tools and equipment. Avoid scraping the turf, chipping at ice or using a metal edge against the surface. Do not add a deicer without checking its compatibility and instructions.

Keep snow from treated paved areas off the turf where possible. Watch for ice during thawing and refreezing, and keep people away from slippery areas. A route to a pet area should remain practical without depending on aggressive scraping.

## Check the surface after the thaw

Remove debris gently and look for displaced infill, opening seams, lifted edges and changes in level. Photograph new conditions and compare them with any earlier records. A ripple or wet spot does not reveal which layer needs work without assessment.

Ask the installer before adding infill or attempting a repair. Keep the product instructions with the [ongoing care plan](/blog/how-long-does-artificial-turf-last/).

## Winter planning checklist

- Mark drainage routes and usual snow-storage areas.
- Identify the paths that need to remain accessible.
- Obtain the product’s snow and ice guidance.
- Use compatible tools without scraping the surface.
- Record and assess changes after thawing.

Bring those site details to a [Denver metro installation visit](/denver-metro/) so the preparation and access can be discussed before pricing.
