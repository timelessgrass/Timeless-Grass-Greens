## Follow the water beyond the backing

Ask for a description of the proposed layers and how water will move through or away from them. The turf’s rating applies to the product under its test conditions; the ground below may absorb water at a different rate.

Existing drains, finished levels and neighboring surfaces also matter. Show the installer where downspouts discharge and where water currently collects. A new surface should not be used as an assumed cure for an unresolved drainage problem.

## Give the assessment useful information

Photograph the yard after rain and note when each image was taken. Mark persistent wet spots, low areas, property boundaries and any known drainage features or easements on a simple plan. Share previous reports or drainage work if you have them.

A bucket of water on one spot does not establish the capacity or cause of a site-wide problem. Ask what investigation is needed for the proposed scope and have any additional drainage work described and priced separately.

## Keep the route open after installation

Follow the product’s debris removal and maintenance guidance. Watch edges, drains and low areas after storms and record new changes. Avoid washing displaced infill into the surrounding yard or a drain.

If puddles recur, have the area assessed before deciding which layer needs repair. The backing, base, ground and water arriving from elsewhere all belong in that discussion.

## Drainage questions for the estimate

- What happens to rain after it passes through the turf?
- What existing conditions were observed or tested?
- What preparation or additional drainage is proposed?
- How are adjoining areas and property features protected?
- What care keeps the system working as intended?

Physical drainage and official stormwater classification are related but separate questions. The [impervious-surface guide](/guides/is-artificial-turf-impervious/) covers the documentation to discuss with the relevant office.
