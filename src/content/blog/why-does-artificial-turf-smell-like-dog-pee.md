## Follow the pattern of use

Note where the dog spends time and where the smell is strongest. Favorite corners, the edge beside a fence and a route from the door may receive different use from the open lawn. Record when the odor returns after cleaning and the products and methods already tried.

Artificial turf still needs pet-waste removal and cleaning. A product marketed for pets should not be treated as odor-proof. Use the [pet-turf cleaning routine](/blog/how-to-clean-pet-turf/) as a starting point, then follow the directions for the actual surface and cleaner.

## Check cleaning coverage and drainage

Make sure the routine covers the affected area and that waste or debris is not left around edges. Follow the cleaner’s dilution, contact-time and rinsing directions rather than repeatedly increasing its strength.

Record any persistent water or slow-draining areas and show them to the installer or care provider. The turf backing is only one part of the drainage route. A product drainage rating alone does not establish how the installed system and ground below behave.

## Treat infill claims as product questions

Ask what the selected infill is intended to do and how it should be maintained. Odor-control materials do not remove the need for waste pickup and cleaning. Laboratory performance of an ingredient does not guarantee that a particular dog run will remain free of odor.

Before adding or replacing infill, confirm compatibility with the turf and any applicable material requirements. Different products should not be mixed simply because both are advertised for pets.

## If the odor persists

- Record the locations, timing and use pattern.
- List the cleaners and methods already tried.
- Photograph any water that remains after rain or rinsing.
- Request an assessment of the routine and installed system.
- Ask why any proposed repair is needed and what would remain.

For [a new pet area](/services/pet-turf/), include access for cleanup and the dog’s daily route in the layout. For an existing area, agree the identified problem and scope before choosing intensive cleaning, repair or replacement.
