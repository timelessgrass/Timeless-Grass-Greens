## Compare the complete pet-turf option

Ask whether the proposed turf uses infill and what material and amount are specified. Include the backing, base and drainage route in the comparison. A product promoted for dogs should come with a clear care routine for the whole system.

Describe the number of dogs, their preferred spots and whether the area is a private run or shared space. Show how you will reach it for waste pickup and cleaning. Those practical details belong alongside the material selection.

## Ask what supports an odor or cooling claim

Request documentation for the actual product, the conditions under which it was tested and the maintenance it needs. A laboratory result for an ingredient does not establish how an installed dog run will smell or feel in use.

Turf can become hot in direct sun. Plan shade and an alternative resting area rather than relying on an infill label to settle the heat concern. The [heat article](/blog/how-hot-does-artificial-turf-get/) explains what to consider.

## Check applicable material limits

On Florida single-family lots within the state rule’s scope, infill choices are subject to its material requirements. The [Florida infill guide](/guides/florida-turf-infill-rules/) explains the natural-material provision and limited synthetic-infill exception. Confirm the proposed product rather than assuming every “pet” infill qualifies.

For an existing installation, identify what is already there before topping up or changing materials. Have recurring loss, odor or drainage problems assessed instead of mixing in a new product as an assumed fix.

## A product comparison checklist

- Exact turf and specified infill, if used.
- Compatibility with the backing and intended use.
- Applicable material documentation.
- Cleaning instructions and access needs.
- Evidence behind any odor-control or cooling claim.
- Method for inspecting and maintaining the installed infill.
