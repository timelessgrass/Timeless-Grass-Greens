## The short version

- **South Carolina:** no carpet or wood inside the required deck width, and the deck must be non-slip and impervious.
- **Florida:** the wet deck must be concrete or another nonabsorbent material with a slip-resistant finish. So must the first 15 feet of the walkway to the restrooms.
- **North Carolina (Brunswick County):** every deck and walkway must have a slip-resistant, impervious surface.

None of these rules names artificial turf. Whether a synthetic surface could ever count as pool deck is the health department's call, so plan your turf for the ground beyond the deck. This is a summary of the rule texts as posted in September 2026, not legal advice.

## Is your pool a public pool?

A hotel, condo or community pool usually is, even when only residents and their guests swim there.

South Carolina's [Regulation 61-51](https://des.sc.gov/sites/des/files/Library/Regulations/R.61-51.pdf) sorts public pools into types. Type B covers pools at hotels, motels, apartments, condominium developments, subdivisions and similar uses. A pool built with a single-family home or duplex, used by that household and its guests, falls outside the definition.

Florida's definition in [Fla. Stat. 514.011](https://www.leg.state.fl.us/Statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0500-0599/0514/Sections/0514.011.html) lists pools serving subdivisions and cooperative living projects of five or more units, such as apartments, hotels and townhouses. Smaller associations get a carve-out: [Fla. Stat. 514.0115](https://www.leg.state.fl.us/Statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0500-0599/0514/Sections/0514.0115.html) exempts pools serving HOAs of no more than 32 units or parcels, not run as public lodging, from most state supervision. Water quality still applies. Ask your county health department which side your pool sits on before you rely on it.

North Carolina's rules apply to public swimming pools as state law defines them. Your local health department can tell you whether a community pool counts.

## South Carolina: keep the minimum deck clear

Section C.6 of Regulation 61-51 sets the deck. It must run continuously around the pool, unobstructed, at least:

- **Type A** (open to the general public): 6 feet.
- **Type B** (hotels, condos, subdivisions): 4 feet, or 6 feet for a pool over 1,600 square feet.

That deck must be broom-finish concrete or a material equal in strength and durability. It has to be "non-slip, impervious and no hazard to bare feet," sloped away from the pool. Then the line that matters most here: "No wood decking or carpet is allowed within the required minimum deck widths."

A few more lines shape where your turf can start:

- **Equipment needs room.** At least 3 feet of deck on the sides and rear of diving or slide equipment, lifeguard chairs, ladders and handrails.
- **Planters count as obstructions.** Up to 10% of the perimeter may be obstructed, with the required deck width behind or through the obstruction within 15 feet of the water.
- **Fence height is measured from outside.** A Type B pool needs a fence at least 4 feet tall, measured from the exterior of the pool area. If turf goes in outside the fence, measure again from the finished surface.

Changing a permitted deck takes paperwork. The regulation's definition of "alteration" includes deck resurfacing, and no public pool may be altered until SCDES issues a permit to construct. Section I adds that a change order request for deck work must be approved in writing before work starts.

## Florida: the wet deck and the walk to the restrooms

Florida sets public pool construction in the Florida Building Code, Section 454.1. The Department of Health's [pool checklist for the 8th edition](https://www.floridahealth.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/poolchecklistfbc8thed9-1-24.docx), dated 29 August 2024, quotes the deck requirements:

- **Material.** Wet decks "shall be constructed of concrete or other nonabsorbent material having a smooth slip resistant finish." Wooden decks and walkways are prohibited (454.1.3.1.1).
- **Width.** At least 4 feet unobstructed around the pool, curb, ladders, handrails, diving boards and slides (454.1.3.1.3).
- **Slope.** 2% to 4%, away from the pool or to deck drains. If settling causes standing water, the slope must be restored or drains added (454.1.3.1.2).
- **Walkways.** The route between the pool and the restrooms must meet the same material standard for its first 15 feet from the nearest water's edge (454.1.3.1.5).
- **Obstructions.** Up to 20% of the perimeter, none longer than 20 feet where the water is 5 feet deep or less, with a walk behind it within 15 feet of the water (454.1.3.1.6).

One water rule reaches past the deck. Florida's pool rule, [Chapter 64E-9](https://www.floridahealth.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/64e-9faceff7-20-2016a004eff6-11-2023.doc), says landscape irrigation water that wets the wet deck or the pool must be potable, or tested to potable quality. Reclaimed water used inside the pool fence or within 100 feet of the water must go through drip irrigation or soaker hoses. If you plan to rinse turf beside the deck, ask the county health department how that applies.

Then the order of steps. The [Department of Health](https://www.floridahealth.gov/community-environmental-public-health/environmental-public-health/water-quality/public-swimming-pools/) says an operating permit application must reach it before the building department can issue a construction or modification permit for a public pool. Chapter 64E-9 applies that sequence to modifications, filed on form DH 4159.

## Brunswick County, North Carolina

The North Carolina end of the Grand Strand works under [15A NCAC 18A .2522](http://reports.oah.state.nc.us/ncac/title%2015a%20-%20environmental%20quality/chapter%2018%20-%20environmental%20health/subchapter%20a/15a%20ncac%2018a%20.2522.pdf). An outdoor pool needs a continuous deck with at least 6 feet of clear walking space, or 8 feet where the swimming area is 1,600 square feet or more. Spas need 4 feet around at least half their edge.

The surface rule is wider than South Carolina's. "All decks and walkways shall have a slip-resistant, impervious surface." It isn't limited to the minimum width, so a pool walkway across the lawn is covered too.

Deck work can be more than upkeep. The [definitions rule](http://reports.oah.state.nc.us/ncac/title%2015a%20-%20environmental%20quality/chapter%2018%20-%20environmental%20health/subchapter%20a/15a%20ncac%2018a%20.2508.pdf) calls a pool "remodeled" when renovation disrupts the majority of the deck. Deck replacement counts as repair only where the Department has decided nothing underneath needs to change. Plans for a public pool go to the local health department and must be approved before construction ([.2509](http://reports.oah.state.nc.us/ncac/title%2015a%20-%20environmental%20quality/chapter%2018%20-%20environmental%20health/subchapter%20a/15a%20ncac%2018a%20.2509.pdf)).

## Where turf does fit

Leave the required deck alone and turf still has room around a community pool:

- **Lawn and planting beds beyond the deck edge,** where lounge chairs sit on grass today.
- **Play or picnic lawns** outside the deck and clear of any pool walkway.
- **Ground outside the pool fence,** once the fence height is checked from the finished surface.

Plan three things at the edge where deck meets turf:

1. **Deck water.** All three states slope decks away from the pool or toward drains. Where a deck sheds to its outer edge, the turf beside it takes that water.
2. **Heat and the barrier.** Turf beside a pool can get hot in full sun, and a new base can change how a fence measures. Our [guide to turf around a pool](/blog/artificial-turf-around-a-pool/) covers both.
3. **Written approval.** Send the layout to the health department with the deck widths marked, and keep the answer on file.

## Before you order turf

1. Confirm with the health department that your pool is public, and which type it is.
2. Measure the required deck width for that type, plus the extra space around ladders, rails, lifeguard chairs and slides.
3. Mark every pool walkway: the first 15 feet to the restrooms in Florida, all of them in North Carolina.
4. Keep turf, planters and furniture off the required deck.
5. Note which way the deck slopes and where its water leaves.
6. Ask in writing whether the work needs a change order, permit or plan approval.
7. Get that answer before material is ordered.
