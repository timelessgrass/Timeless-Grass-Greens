## Pick the spot before the puppy comes home

Housetraining advice starts in the same place: one bathroom spot, used every time. [Humane World for Animals](https://www.humaneworld.org/en/resources/how-housetrain-your-dog-or-puppy) says to choose a consistent spot outside and always take your puppy there on a leash. The [UC Davis veterinary behavior handout](https://www.vetmed.ucdavis.edu/sites/g/files/dgvnsk491/files/inline-files/Housetraining_Your_Puppy.pdf) says to take the puppy to the same area every time, with no play until it has gone.

On a turf yard, one spot does a second job. It keeps the waste in one place, so the daily pickup and rinse cover a few square feet instead of the whole lawn.

Choose it with three things in mind:

- **Close to the door you use.** A young puppy goes out first thing in the morning, last thing at night and many times between.
- **Within reach of a hose.** You will rinse this spot more than anywhere else in the yard.
- **Draining away from the house.** Rinse water should run away from the walls, not toward them. Our article on [turf next to the house](/blog/artificial-turf-next-to-the-house/) covers the slope and the buffer.

If snow lands on the turf in the Denver metro, clear a path to the spot and the spot itself. Our [Colorado winter article](/blog/does-artificial-turf-hold-up-in-colorado-winters/) covers how to do that without scraping out the infill.

## Take the puppy out on a schedule

Puppies can't hold it long. The [American Kennel Club](https://www.akc.org/expert-advice/training/how-to-potty-train-a-puppy/) says most puppies come home at about 8 weeks old, when bladder control is at its weakest, and that a puppy can generally hold it for about as many hours as its age in months. Humane World advises a trip outside at least every two hours, and especially after the puppy wakes, plays, eats or drinks.

The UC Davis handout gives a schedule by age:

- **6 to 14 weeks:** 8 to 10 trips a day
- **14 to 20 weeks:** 6 to 8 trips a day
- **20 to 30 weeks:** 4 to 6 trips a day
- **30 weeks and older:** 3 to 4 trips a day

Each trip follows the same pattern:

1. **Leash on, straight to the spot.** Humane World says to keep the puppy on a leash in the yard during housetraining, treating the yard like any other room in the house.
2. **Say the cue.** The AKC suggests a calm phrase such as "go potty" as the puppy starts, so it links the words to the act.
3. **Reward the finish.** Praise and a small treat, right away. Humane World says to wait until the puppy has completely finished, because a distracted puppy may stop midway.
4. **Then play.** UC Davis puts going first and playtime second.

On turf, the leash also decides where you will be cleaning. A puppy loose on the lawn picks its own places, and each one needs a rinse.

## Keep the spot inviting without letting it smell

Smell is one reason a puppy goes back to the same place. The AKC says dogs frequently return to areas that still smell like earlier accidents, and recommends an enzymatic cleaner to remove that smell. Indoors, that is exactly what you want. On the potty spot, in the first weeks, it works against you.

So split the cleaning by where it happens:

- **On the potty spot, plain water.** Rinse it at the end of each day. Wash down the whole area once a week; [UF/IFAS Extension](https://ask.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/EP612) notes that many manufacturers recommend weekly wash-downs to remove pet waste and its odor.
- **Everywhere else, enzyme cleaner.** Use it indoors, and on any part of the turf the puppy used by mistake. Check first that the product suits your turf; our [pet turf cleaning guide](/blog/how-to-clean-pet-turf/) covers what to look for.

You can also move the scent on purpose. Humane World says to put the paper towels from an indoor accident in the potty spot, so the puppy links that smell with the right place. UC Davis suggests leaving feces in the right area, when appropriate, for the same reason.

On turf, keep that to hours, not days. The [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/toxocariasis/spreads/index.html) says pet waste should be picked up daily. Puppies usually get roundworm from their mother, and from 3 to 4 weeks old the worms lay eggs that pass in the stool. Those eggs need 2 to 4 weeks in the environment to become infective, and can then survive for months or even years. Leaving a stool until the next trip and bagging it the same day still meets the CDC's daily pickup. Leaving it all week does not. The CDC also advises having a veterinarian treat young dogs for worms.

Once the puppy uses the spot reliably, treat it like any pet area: same-day rinses, a weekly wash-down and an enzyme cleaner on the spot when it needs one. If the spot still smells after rinsing, look at drainage and infill. Our article on [why turf smells like dog pee](/blog/why-does-artificial-turf-smell-like-dog-pee/) explains what to check.

## When accidents happen

Expect them. Humane World calls accidents a normal part of training, and UC Davis tells you not to get discouraged.

- **Caught in the act.** The [ASPCA](https://www.aspca.org/news/house-training-your-dog-or-puppy) says it's fine to startle the puppy by clapping, then get it outside. Take it to the turf spot and reward it if it finishes there.
- **Found afterward.** Just clean it up. The ASPCA says a dog learns nothing from being scolded for a past accident, "even one a few minutes old."
- **Never punish.** Humane World says scolding or rubbing a puppy's nose in it only makes it fearful and slows progress. UC Davis warns that punishment may lead to more serious behavior problems.
- **Clean it thoroughly** with an enzymatic cleaner, as UC Davis advises, then take the paper towel out to the spot.

The same steps apply to an accident on the turf away from the spot. Rinse it, use an enzyme cleaner that suits your turf, and shorten the time between trips; Humane World suggests shorter gaps between breaks after an accident. If a puppy stays hard to housetrain despite a consistent routine, the AKC says it should see a veterinarian.

## What the turf needs under a puppy

A potty spot puts a puppy's urine on a few square feet, many times a day, for months. It has to leave, not sit. UF/IFAS Extension describes the route: the waste "must pass through the artificial turf, the underlying substrate, and the weed barrier" before it is carried away. Every layer has to pass water.

- **A backing that drains.** To qualify for Thornton's rebate, turf needs a meshed or hole-punched backing, with holes in a grid no more than 4 by 6 inches on center, designed to drain at least two inches an hour ([Thornton Water](https://www.thorntonwater.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/2026-residential_artificial_turf_info__rules-1.pdf)). Florida's standard for single-family lots of an acre or less requires permeable turf on a permeable backing with a pervious subgrade ([Rule 62-308.100](https://flrules.org/gateway/readFile.asp?sid=0&tid=30839130&type=1&file=62-308.100.doc)).
- **A base that drains.** Thornton calls for at least three inches of compacted aggregate. Florida requires natural subgrade material, washed so the fines don't bind, graded for positive drainage and installed to prevent pooling.
- **A product made for dogs.** The [Synthetic Turf Council](https://cdn.ymaws.com/www.syntheticturfcouncil.org/resource/resmgr/guidelines/STC_Considerations_When_Buyi.pdf) says its buying guidelines for landscape turf may not apply to dog runs. Ask for the product's pet-use documentation and drainage rate in writing, in any of our three markets.
- **Infill that fits the yard.** Our [infill article](/blog/what-infill-works-best-for-dogs/) compares the options. In Florida, the rule allows natural infill such as clean sand, or coated sand with a non-toxic coating, and keeps rubber to the footprint of playground equipment.
- **Edges a puppy can't lift.** Thornton's rules and Florida's both require turf anchored at all edges and seams. Our article on [dogs that dig and chew](/blog/pet-turf-for-dogs-that-dig/) covers fence lines and supervision.

Hold the turf to that before the puppy arrives. A spot that drains is easier to keep fresh than one you are trying to clean your way out of.

## A puppy plan for a turf yard

1. Pick one spot near the door, within reach of a hose and draining away from the house.
2. Take the puppy there on a leash at least every two hours, and after sleep, meals, drinks and play.
3. Say the cue, reward the finish, then play.
4. Bag stools the same day and rinse the spot with plain water daily.
5. Use enzyme cleaner on mistakes, not on the spot, until the habit sticks.
6. Never punish an accident. Clean it and shorten the gaps.
7. Check that the backing and base drain before the puppy comes home.
