## Start with the putts you want to practice

Decide whether the emphasis is short putts, longer pace control, breaking lines or a mix. Include the people who will use it and the available space around the cups. A surface that is faster for its own sake may make some small layouts less useful.

Ask your regular course about its usual conditions if you want a familiar reference. Describe how you would like the green to play rather than treating a number found online as the specification.

## Understand what the number measures

A Stimpmeter reading is a ball-roll distance measured using a defined procedure. It helps compare conditions when the measurement is taken correctly. The selected area and direction of measurement matter, particularly on a small contoured green.

Use the instrument’s instructions or have the installer demonstrate the assessment. Avoid treating an informal downhill roll as an equivalent speed reading. Keep a few repeatable practice lines as a practical way to notice later changes.

## Design speed and shape together

A faster surface makes the effect of slope more pronounced, so discuss where putts should break and where balls should settle near a cup. A compact green needs useful hole positions and room to practice, as well as attractive contours.

Ask how the selected product, infill and ongoing care relate to the intended roll. Keep that explanation with the layout so future brushing or infill work is guided by the actual system. The [putting-green care article](/blog/putting-green-care/) covers what to check when the roll changes.

## Planning checklist

- The putts and practice distances you want.
- A familiar course or other useful starting reference.
- Contours and usable cup locations.
- The proposed surface and care requirements.
- A clear way to assess and record the finished roll.
