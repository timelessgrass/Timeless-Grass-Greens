## Plan the tent before the turf

If your event lawn will ever hold a tent, know that the tent rules and the turf rules pull in different directions. Settle them before the base goes in.

The fire code wants the tent held down. Section 3103.9 of the [2021 International Fire Code](https://codes.iccsafe.org/content/IFC2021P2/chapter-31-tents-temporary-special-event-structures-and-other-membrane-structures) says a temporary tent must be designed and installed to withstand the weather and not collapse, with documentation of its stability given to the fire code official. Stakes are the tent industry's cheaper option: a 2019 article in [InTents](https://intentsmag.com/2019/08/01/study-time-ifai-tools-for-better-staking-and-ballasting/), the Advanced Textiles Association's tent magazine, calls staking much more cost-efficient than ballast.

The turf industry says the opposite. For special events on sports fields, the Synthetic Turf Council's voluntary [maintenance guidelines](https://cdn.ymaws.com/staging-stc.site-ym.com/resource/resmgr/guidelines/STC_Guidelines_for_Maintenan.pdf) say no anchoring spikes, posts or footings should be driven into the turf. And tent stakes go deep. The industry's [pocket guide to stake pullout capacity](https://hse.kaust.edu.sa/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/staking-pocket-guide.pdf) uses a 1-inch steel stake driven 36 inches as its baseline. That is ground penetration, which the industry's 811 campaign warns can strike gas lines, water pipes and electrical cables.

There are three ways to reconcile the two:

1. **Ballast on the turf.** The tent professionals interviewed by InTents say ballast usually fails by sliding or by being too light. They advise keeping it close to the tent leg, and say rubber pads or plywood under it reduce sliding. The industry's ballasting tool estimates friction for common ballast on a range of surfaces, so ask your tent company which surface it assumed.
2. **A border that takes stakes.** Guy ropes run out past the tent walls. Keep that ring off the turf, on ground that can be staked once the lines are marked.
3. **Anchors built in first.** If the same tent goes up in the same spot each season, ask the tent maker whether fixed anchors set before the turf would meet its requirement. It's the same logic as [pergola footings](/blog/pergola-posts-and-artificial-turf/).

Before any stake goes in, the [Call Before You Stake](https://tent.textiles.org/811-call-before-you-stake/) campaign from the Advanced Textiles Association's Tent Rental Division and the Common Ground Alliance says the installer should contact 811 at least 2 to 3 business days ahead. Mark the lines you own on the turf plan too, such as sprinkler pipe and [lighting wire](/blog/landscape-lighting-wire-under-artificial-turf/).

## Leave room for the clearances

A tent needs clear space around it, and that space shapes the turf layout. The 2021 International Fire Code and [North Carolina's 2018 fire code](https://codes.iccsafe.org/content/NCFC2018/chapter-31-tents-and-other-membrane-structures) both set these:

- **20 feet from lot lines, buildings, other tents, parked vehicles and engines.** Guy ropes and wires count as part of the tent. Some exceptions apply.
- **A 12-foot fire break** on all sides, free of guy ropes, unless the fire code official approves otherwise.
- **No open flame or cooking device** inside the tent or within 20 feet while it's open to the public, unless approved. Generators stay at least 20 feet away, behind a fence or enclosure.
- **Combustible vegetation cleared** from the tent area and for 30 feet around it. North Carolina's text says readily ignitable vegetation.

Florida builds its fire code on NFPA 1 and NFPA 101 under [Fla. Stat. 633.202](https://www.leg.state.fl.us/Statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0600-0699/0633/Sections/0633.202.html), and the wording changes. St. Johns County's [tent permit form](https://www.sjcfl.us/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/Tent-Application.pdf), citing NFPA 101, asks for 10 feet between stake lines. It says the ground inside the tent and within 10 feet of it must be clear of vegetation and combustible materials, and it allows no cooking of any kind under a tent.

Turf is plastic. Our article on [whether turf can catch fire](/blog/can-artificial-turf-catch-fire/) explains what its spec-sheet fire test does and doesn't show. None of these code texts names artificial turf, so ask the fire marshal how it treats a turf lawn under and around a tent. Both codes also want a certificate, before a permit is granted, that a tent's floor coverings pass an NFPA 701 flame test. Ask whether that reaches a permanent lawn, and bring the turf's spec sheet.

## Who signs off on the tent

The fire department that serves the venue issues the permit, and the code it enforces sets the trigger.

| Code | Where it applies | A tent needs the fire marshal | Open-sided exception |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2021 International Fire Code | The ICC's model code | Over 400 square feet | Up to 700 square feet, 12 feet from structures and other tents |
| 2018 North Carolina fire code | Brunswick County | Over 800 square feet | Up to 1,800 square feet with at least 75% of the perimeter open, 12 feet clear |
| Florida Fire Prevention Code | Northeast Florida | Over 900 square feet; smaller tents are exempt by statute | None listed on the St. Johns County form |

- **North Carolina.** The [State Fire Marshal's office](https://www.ncosfm.gov/codes/codes-current-and-past) lists the 2018 codes as currently effective and has posted an update on when the 2024 code takes effect. Ask which one applies on your date.
- **South Carolina.** [State law](https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t06c009.php) has the building codes council adopt the International Code Council's fire code, with amendments. Ask your city or Horry County which edition applies.
- **Denver metro.** Ask the city fire department or fire protection district that serves the venue which edition it has adopted.

Both ICC codes above also want a detailed site and floor plan with the application for a tent holding 50 or more people, showing exits, seating, and heating and electrical equipment.

## Chairs, aisles and heels

Rows of chairs and tables load the turf in small spots. For events on sports fields, the STC guidelines call for plywood, interlocking plastic panels or similar weight-spreading materials under all chairs and tables. Avoid panels with a ridged profile, which transfers onto the turf. The guidelines also warn that improper plastic covers can cause heat damage, so ask the turf maker how long flooring can stay down in the sun.

Traffic moves infill. High-use areas lose it faster, and brushing, drag mats and rakes spread it back, so brush the aisle and the bar line after each event.

Heels are a firmness question. The [Access Board's guide](https://www.access-board.gov/ada/guides/chapter-3-floor-and-ground-surfaces/) to accessible routes says a firm surface resists deforming under applied force. Inside a tent, both fire codes call for smooth-surfaced, unobstructed aisles at least 44 inches wide in public areas. Put the ceremony aisle, the bar line and the walk to the restrooms on pavers or another firm surface, and test a turf sample in a narrow heel. Our [wheelchair access article](/blog/is-artificial-turf-wheelchair-accessible/) goes further.

## Spills and the morning after

The STC guidelines say most stains come out with hot, not boiling, water and a household detergent your turf's builder approves, then a rinse. Keep cleaners with alcohol or acetone solvents off the turf, and freeze gum with ice to chip it off. Our [stain removal guide](/blog/how-to-remove-stains-from-artificial-turf/) covers gum, oil and paint. When event flooring comes up, groom the area and sweep it with a magnet for dropped nails and screws.

The fire codes add rules for the event itself. The floor inside a tent and the ground for 30 feet around it must be kept free of combustible waste, removed at least once a day while the public uses the tent. Smoking isn't allowed in tents. At outdoor assembly events the 2021 code limits it to designated areas, so put that spot on paving, away from the turf.

A brewery that lets dogs onto a Florida patio has its own permit duties, covered in our [dog-friendly restaurant patio article](/blog/turf-for-a-dog-friendly-restaurant-patio-florida/).

## Summer heat and event-day rain

**Heat.** Penn State researchers [found](https://plantscience.psu.edu/research/centers/ssrc/documents/temperature-irrigation.pdf) synthetic turf substantially hotter than natural turfgrass in sunlight. Watering cooled every synthetic surface they tested. Temperatures rebounded somewhat after about 15 minutes, and on one test day the watered plots were still cooler more than three hours later. Watering before dawn did little by the afternoon. So water shortly before guests arrive, and count the tent as shade. Our article on [how hot turf gets](/blog/how-hot-does-artificial-turf-get/) has more.

**Rain.** The staking guide says stakes in saturated ground hold about half of what they hold in the same ground dry. The InTents experts advise testing the soil before the job and again on event day. The lawn's own drainage depends on its base and the soil under it, as our article on [how rain drains through turf](/blog/does-rain-drain-through-artificial-turf/) explains.

At a hotel, a lawn beside the pool has deck rules of its own. Our [hotel and HOA pool deck article](/blog/artificial-turf-at-a-public-pool-deck/) sets them out by state.

## Event lawn checklist

1. Ask the fire department which code applies and at what tent size its permit starts.
2. Get the tent maker's anchoring requirement in writing.
3. Choose ballast, a stake border or built-in anchors before the turf goes in.
4. Have the installer contact 811 before any stake, and map the lines you own.
5. Draw the code's clearances around the tent zone.
6. Ask how the fire marshal treats turf under and around a tent.
7. Order flat floor panels for chairs and tables.
8. Build firm paths for the aisle, the bar and the restrooms.
9. On hot days, water shortly before guests arrive.
