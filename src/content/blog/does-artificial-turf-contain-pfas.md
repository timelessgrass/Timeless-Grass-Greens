## Ask about the product you are actually buying

A statement about a brand or an older product is not enough to identify what is in the proposed system. Request the product name, backing, infill and the date of the documentation. If a substitute is offered later, repeat that check for the substitute.

PFAS have been detected in some turf components. Those findings cannot identify the contents of a different product, so match the documentation to the materials being supplied.

## Understand the wording

A manufacturer may state that PFAS were not intentionally added during production. A laboratory report may state that specified chemicals were not detected under a particular method and detection limit. Read the wording and scope before treating either as a broader assurance.

A test for selected PFAS cannot establish the absence of all PFAS. Ask which substances or measurements were included, which parts of the turf were sampled and whether the report applies to the current product.

Use the documents to understand the tested materials and the limits of each claim. Material content, exposure and health outcomes are different questions; a product statement or limited test does not resolve the health risk of an individual installation.

## Include applicable material requirements

Ask the installer to identify the documentation needed for the project’s jurisdiction and use. Our [Colorado turf law guide](/guides/colorado-turf-law/) and [Florida material guide](/guides/florida-turf-infill-rules/) explain the relevant local questions. A product selected for one type of property should not automatically be assumed to satisfy another property’s requirements.

## A document checklist

- Exact turf product, backing and proposed infill.
- A dated manufacturer statement with its claim spelled out.
- The report supporting any testing claim.
- The tested components, method and detection limits.
- Confirmation that the supplied materials match the documents.

Request this information with any quote, including a TIMELESS quote. If a document is missing, keep the material question open until it is supplied rather than filling the gap with a verbal assurance.
