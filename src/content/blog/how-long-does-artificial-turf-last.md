## Fields last about a decade. Yards have no official number

Most of the published numbers come from sports fields. New Jersey's environment department summed up the research in a [2025 report](https://dep.nj.gov/wp-content/uploads/dsr/synthetic-turf-report-2025.pdf): with proper maintenance, synthetic turf fields are expected to last about 10 to 12 years, and then the surface has to come out and be replaced.

[One industry estimate](https://sportsvenuecalculator.com/knowledge/artificial-turf-field/turf-field-replacement-and-recycling/) lands in the same range: usually 8 to 10 years for a field, and 10 to 12 with good care.

We found no government or university figure for home lawns. Most yards see far less traffic than a ball field that hosts games every night. They get the same sun, though, and often a dog. So treat the field numbers as a reference point, not a promise, and look at the three things that actually wear turf out.

## Sunlight is the main clock

Turf blades are plastic, mostly polyethylene, and ultraviolet light breaks plastic down. The New Jersey report puts UV radiation first on its list of what degrades turf fibers and infill over time.

How fast depends on what the manufacturer added to protect the plastic. A [UN Environment Programme assessment](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10088630/) reports lab results for high-density polyethylene under accelerated sunlight:

- **No UV protection:** brittle after 300 hours.
- **A UV absorber alone:** stable up to 900 hours.
- **A UV absorber plus light stabilizers:** little degradation out to 1,300 hours.

Those are hours in a test chamber, not years in your yard. But they show why two turfs that look identical on day one can age very differently. Ask what UV protection the fiber has before you choose it.

## Wear and weather do the rest

Sun is not the only thing at work. The New Jersey report lists two more:

- **Mechanical stress.** Running, sliding and jumping break fibers and infill into smaller pieces. So does grooming: the report notes that brushing adds wear too.
- **Weather.** Rain, snow and swings in temperature add their own physical and chemical breakdown.

Infill thins out over time as well. On fields, infill loss has been measured at about 0.8 mm of depth a year after the third or fourth year. Low infill leaves the fibers without support, so they lie flat and wear faster.

A back yard has its own version of all this. The dog's favorite path, the spot by the gate and the strip under the swing wear first, so plan for them at the design stage.

## Seams and edges often go before the fibers

Much of the time, turf isn't worn out when it starts to look bad. A seam has opened, or an edge has lifted. The [Synthetic Turf Council](https://cdn.ymaws.com/staging-stc.site-ym.com/resource/resmgr/guidelines/STC_Suggested_Guidelines_for.pdf), the industry's trade association, suggests inspecting seams and other installation features at least once a year. It says a seam that opens or comes loose may need quick, temporary gluing until it can be properly fixed.

The same guidelines suggest keeping a written log of the care the surface gets, which makes a slow change easier to spot. Catch a lifting seam early and it is a small repair. Leave it, and water, infill and wear get under the edge and it keeps opening.

## Signs your turf is near the end

The industry guide above lists the usual signs:

- **Seams keep loosening** even after repair.
- **The color fades** toward gray-green.
- **Loose fibers and infill pile up** along the edges.
- **Blades split** or break off when you run a hand through them.

Persistent odor and new puddles are also reasons to investigate. Review care, infill condition and drainage before deciding whether the surface, base or another part of the site needs attention.

## What makes turf last longer

Most of the care is simple, and the Synthetic Turf Council's guidelines cover the core of it. Brush the turf now and then to even out low spots in the infill and stand the fibers back up.

1. **Brush it on a schedule.** Light and regular beats heavy and rare, since brushing wears fibers too.
2. **Top up the infill** where it has thinned, so the fibers keep their support.
3. **Fix loose seams and edges early.** A small glue repair now saves a section later.
4. **Clear leaves and debris, gently.** New Jersey's report lists leaf blowing, compared with raking, among the habits that carry infill and plastic bits off a field. Careless snow clearing and piling are on the same list.
5. **Rinse pet areas.** It keeps smells from building in the infill and base.
6. **Spread the wear.** Move a play set or a dog's run now and then if the layout allows.

Before you buy, ask two questions: what UV protection the fiber has, and what the manufacturer's written terms say about fading and wear for the exact product.

## When it is time to replace it

Replacement is not always a full rebuild. If the base is still level, compacted and draining, it can often be regraded and reused, which can reduce the scope compared with rebuilding it.
