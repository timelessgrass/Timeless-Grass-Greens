## Set the pad size first, then place the turf

A fire pit, a freestanding grill or a built-in outdoor kitchen all need the same thing before turf goes anywhere near them: a hardscape pad sized to the appliance's own clearance, not to the appliance's footprint.

That clearance comes from the manufacturer, printed in the installation manual for the specific unit you're buying. It is not a rule of thumb, and it is not the same number from one fire pit or grill to the next. Read the manual before the layout is final, because the pad has to be poured to that number, and turf has to stop outside it.

## What one manufacturer's numbers actually look like

Clearances vary by fuel type, by whether the appliance sits against a wall or stands free, and by what that wall is made of. Two examples, from two different products, show the range.

One outdoor gas fire bowl's installation instructions call for a minimum of 48 inches on every side to any structure or combustible material, and 96 inches of overhead clearance to any structure or combustible material above it. The same manual sets a minimum 6-inch clearance at floor level on all sides.

One built-in gas grill's installation manual sets a different scale entirely, because it's designed to sit inside a kitchen enclosure rather than stand alone. Its side and rear clearances run from 6 inches to a non-combustible wall, up to 15 inches to an unprotected combustible wall, with 12 inches allowed to a protected combustible wall built with a ventilated air gap. Overhead, it calls for a minimum 60 inches to any combustible construction, and requires an exhaust hood if the grill sits under combustible construction at all.

Those two ranges, from two different appliance types, are the reason "well back" isn't a number you can build a pad to. Get the manual for the unit going in, and use its figures.

## What changes near a wetland buffer

Several of our Northeast Florida communities — [Nocatee](/northeast-florida/nocatee-fl/), [World Golf Village](/northeast-florida/world-golf-village-fl/) and [Ponte Vedra Beach](/northeast-florida/ponte-vedra-beach-fl/) among them — back lots onto ponds, wetlands or natural water bodies. That changes the layout question before the appliance clearance ever comes up.

St. Johns County's [Land Development Code](https://www.sjcfl.us/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/Article-IV.pdf), Article IV, Sec. 4.01.06, requires a minimum 25-foot natural vegetative upland buffer between developed areas and a contiguous wetland, measured from the state's jurisdictional wetland line. Along the St. Johns, Matanzas, Guana or Tolomato rivers, that buffer grows to 50 feet. A further 25-foot setback applies beyond the buffer on adjacent parcels.

The code does allow accessory uses — the same category a fire pit or grill pad falls into — inside that outer 25-foot setback, provided nothing encroaches into the buffer itself and the structure has no permanent foundation. A slab-mounted fire pit or a built-in kitchen with a footing is a different question from a freestanding unit on pavers; ask the installer which one your product requires, and confirm the buffer line with the county before the pad location is fixed.

Outside St. Johns County, the same idea still applies even though the numbers change: a pond, wetland or drainage easement on the lot line can carry its own setback from the county or the community's own governing documents, separate from whatever the appliance manual requires. Check both before the pad goes in.

## Questions to settle before the layout is final

1. **What does the specific appliance's manual require?** Pull the installation manual for the exact fire pit, grill or kitchen unit before ordering turf, not after.
2. **Is the wall it sits against combustible or protected?** The clearance can change by more than double depending on the answer.
3. **Does the unit need overhead clearance, an exhaust hood, or both?** That governs whether a covered patio or pergola over the pad is possible at all.
4. **Does the lot back onto a wetland, pond or waterway?** If so, confirm the buffer and setback lines with St. Johns County before the pad's location is set, not after.
5. **Is the pad sized for the full kitchen, not just the grill box?** A rotisserie outlet, side burner or lighting each need their own placement inside the same enclosure.
6. **Where does the turf edge land relative to the pad?** The pad's edge, not the appliance, is what the turf has to respect.
