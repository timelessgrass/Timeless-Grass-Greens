## The decision: turf is a separate line item, not a compliance path

If a new building or a roof job in Denver is large enough to trigger the Green Buildings Ordinance, the green-space option is one of several ways to comply — and artificial turf doesn't satisfy it. The ordinance's own definition of green space is "any area that is proposed to contain trees, groundcover, shrubs, urban agriculture, natural grass/turf, or vegetated roofs" (Section 1.03). Every item on that list is a living plant or a planted system. Synthetic turf isn't named, and the natural-grass category that is named comes with its own cap.

That doesn't take turf off the table for a rooftop deck or a podium amenity space. It means the project has two separate questions to answer: how the building satisfies the ordinance (green space, solar, an energy path, or one of the other compliance options), and where a turf amenity area sits relative to that compliance footprint. Settling that split before the layout is drawn keeps a later design change from creating a shortfall the building has to fix.

## What triggers the ordinance, and what the green-space option actually requires

The Rules and Regulations apply to new buildings or additions of 25,000 square feet or more of gross floor area, and to existing buildings of that size when a roof replacement or roof recover covers more than 5 percent of the total roof area or an individual roof section (Sections 3.01 and 4.01). The Building Official has sole responsibility for deciding whether a given project is covered or exempt (Section 2.01) — that determination, not a general read of the ordinance, is where a project's obligations actually start.

Owners who choose the green-space compliance path have to hit one of two coverage figures, set out in Section 4.02(a):

- **New buildings:** 10 percent of gross floor area, or 60 percent of total roof area.
- **Existing buildings replacing the whole roof:** 2 percent of gross floor area, or 18 percent of total roof area. Replacing a single roof section uses the same percentages applied to that section's share of the building.

All green space counted this way has to sit outdoors — vegetation inside a building doesn't count (Section 4.02(c)) — and it has to be maintained for the life of the building. If it dies, the ordinance requires it to be replaced or another compliance option chosen (Section 4.02(b)(vii)). That's a standing maintenance obligation attached to whatever square footage the site development plan shows as the compliance area, which is exactly the reason to keep a synthetic turf amenity deck off that plan.

## Why natural turfgrass has a ceiling, and synthetic turf has none of it

Even where the green-space path uses living turfgrass — sod or seeded grass, not synthetic — the ordinance limits how much of the requirement it can cover. Under Section 4.02(e)(iv)(3), turfgrass may meet 25 percent of the green-space requirement, plus another 25 percent where the grass area also functions as a pollution-removal and volume-reduction buffer for adjacent impervious surfaces, under the regional drainage criteria the rules reference for that credit. That's a maximum of half the requirement from turfgrass, with the remainder made up of trees, shrubs, groundcover, urban agriculture or a vegetated roof.

Artificial turf sits entirely outside that structure. It isn't the natural grass/turf the definition names, so it doesn't get a percentage of the requirement at all — it gets none. Anywhere the ordinance's coverage math is being done, a rooftop or podium turf deck is square footage that has to come from outside the calculation, not a discount inside it.

## If the building uses a vegetated roof, what that path requires

Where a project does put a vegetated roof toward the green-space requirement, the ordinance sets specific technical standards for it, separate from anything a turf installer plans around. Vegetated roofs on commercial buildings need a dedicated Green Roof Construction Permit issued to a licensed Green Roof contractor, on top of the roof permit for the waterproof membrane underneath (Section 4.02(d)(i)(1)(a)) — and the Denver license that contractor has to hold is a separate requirement (Section 2.03(e)). The vegetative system itself has to include a minimum 5-inch depth of growing media, unless a different depth is demonstrated and approved, and the vegetation has to cover 80 percent of the roof within 3 years of the certificate of occupancy (Section 4.02(d)(vi)). The Existing Buildings Option A guide, revised May 18, 2026, lists both figures again in its submittal checklist for roof-permit projects.

None of that applies to a synthetic turf deck that isn't part of the compliance area — a turf deck doesn't need growing media, a plant-coverage timeline, or a green roof contractor's permit. It does still need its own structural and drainage answers, which our [balcony and roof turf article](/blog/artificial-turf-on-a-balcony-or-roof/) covers: confirming the deck's design load with a structural engineer, and keeping drains and scuppers clear of infill and backing.

## What to settle before the project is final

1. **Has the Building Official confirmed whether this project is subject to the ordinance,** and if so, which compliance option the building is using?
2. **If the green-space option is in play, is it meeting the requirement with trees, shrubs, groundcover, urban agriculture or a vegetated roof** — and if turfgrass is part of the mix, does it stay inside the 25-plus-25 percent cap?
3. **Where does the site development plan show the compliance green space,** and is the turf amenity area drawn outside that footprint, on its own line?
4. **If a vegetated roof is part of the plan, who holds the Green Roof Construction Permit,** and has a maintenance plan been submitted for the plant coverage and irrigation requirements?
5. **Has a structural engineer signed off on the turf deck's load,** independent of whatever the compliance area requires?

## Before you finalize the layout

- Confirm the project's ordinance status and compliance option with Community Planning and Development.
- Mark the green-space compliance footprint on the site development plan, separate from any turf amenity area.
