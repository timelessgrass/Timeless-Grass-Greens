## The decision: ask about the fire area before you sign

If you're leasing or building an indoor training facility, fieldhouse or academy with an open turf floor, one question decides a chunk of your build-out budget before you ever pick a turf system: does this space have to be sprinklered throughout? That isn't a turf question. It's a building-code question, and it turns on the size and occupant load of the "fire area" the space sits in, not on what surface covers the floor.

Get the answer from the building official, in writing, before you commit to a lease or price a build-out. A candidate building that looks perfect on square footage alone can cross the sprinkler line the moment you add up the occupant load of the full training floor, or if your suite sits on any floor other than the one people use to leave the building.

## What the code requires, and where it says so

Indoor training space without fixed spectator seating — a gym, an academy floor, an open turf facility used for practice rather than watching a game — is classified Group A-3 under the building code's assembly-use categories. The 2021 International Building Code, as adopted in Colorado, defines Group A-3 as assembly uses for "worship, recreation or amusement" not classified elsewhere, and its own examples list gymnasiums, indoor pools and indoor tennis courts "without spectator seating" ([Chapter 3, Section 303](https://up.codes/viewer/colorado/ibc-2021/chapter/3/occupancy-classification-and-use), checked 14 September 2026) — the same category our [indoor turf for a gym](/blog/indoor-turf-for-a-gym/) article walks through in more detail.

[Section 903.2.1.3](https://up.codes/viewer/colorado/ibc-2021/chapter/9/fire-protection-and-life-safety-systems) (checked 14 September 2026) requires an automatic sprinkler system throughout every story containing a Group A-3 fire area where any one of three conditions applies:

- The fire area exceeds 12,000 square feet.
- The fire area has an occupant load of 300 or more.
- The fire area sits on a floor other than the level of exit discharge — meaning any floor other than the one occupants use to leave the building, not just an upper floor.

Any one of the three is enough on its own. A facility can stay well under 12,000 square feet and still be required to sprinkler because its occupant load passes 300, or because the training floor is one level up or down from grade.

If the facility instead has fixed spectator seating for watching an indoor sporting event — an arena, a rink with bleachers — it's classified Group A-4 rather than A-3. The adjacent subsection, [903.2.1.4](https://up.codes/viewer/colorado/ibc-2021/chapter/9/fire-protection-and-life-safety-systems), applies the identical three conditions to A-4, and adds that sprinklers cover not just the story with the A-4 space but every floor down to the level of exit discharge. A training academy with a few rows of parent seating along the sideline is a judgment call for the building official, not something to guess at from a floor plan.

Colorado adopts its own edition of the International Building Code, and every threshold above is quoted from that adopted Colorado text. Other states amend the model code before adopting it, so the section number and the specific numbers can differ outside Colorado. Confirm the adopted edition and section with your local building official before treating these figures as settled anywhere else.

## What a professional install plans around

None of this changes what goes under the turf — but it changes the project's sequence and its budget line for fire protection, and both belong in the plan before turf gets ordered.

**Confirm the fire area before you sign.** The "fire area" is the space bounded by fire walls or the building's exterior, not necessarily your leased suite. If your training floor opens onto a shared floor plate with other tenants inside the same fire-rated boundary, the whole connected area can count toward the 12,000-square-foot and 300-occupant thresholds — even if your own lease line is smaller. Ask the building official how the fire area is drawn for the specific building before assuming your square footage is what gets measured.

**Get the occupant load calculated, not estimated.** Occupant load for an assembly space is a code calculation based on how the floor is used and its exit arrangement, not a headcount you're free to guess. A training floor with open unfixed space can carry a higher occupant load per square foot than one with rows of fixed seating. Ask the building official for the occupant-load determination for your specific layout before you assume you're under 300.

**Check the floor level early.** A space at grade, on the level occupants use to exit, is judged differently from one that isn't. If your candidate space is upstairs, downstairs, or below grade in the building, that alone can put you in the sprinkler-required column regardless of size — worth knowing before you tour a second-floor space and fall for the price.

**Coordinate sprinklers with your finish selection.** If sprinklers are required, that decision cuts the other way for the turf itself: a building sprinklered throughout unlocks a lower interior-finish flame-spread class for the turf, which our [indoor turf for a gym](/blog/indoor-turf-for-a-gym/) piece and our [can artificial turf catch fire](/blog/can-artificial-turf-catch-fire/) article both cover. Settling the sprinkler question first tells you which finish class the turf actually has to meet.

## Questions to settle before the project is final

Before you sign a lease, buy a building or lock a floor plan for an indoor turf facility, get written answers to:

- What is the fire area for this specific space, and does it include square footage beyond my leased suite?
- What is the calculated occupant load for the training floor as I plan to use it?
- Is the space on the level of exit discharge, or on another floor?
- Which subsection applies — A-3 or A-4 — based on whether the layout includes fixed spectator seating?
- If sprinklers are required, is the building already sprinklered throughout, or does that scope need to be added to the build-out?

Every one of those is the building official's call, not ours and not a leasing agent's. Get it in writing before turf gets ordered.

## A checklist before you book

1. **Pull the fire area and occupancy classification** for the specific space from the building official, not from a floor-plan guess.
2. **Get the occupant-load calculation** in writing for your intended layout.
3. **Confirm the floor level** relative to the level of exit discharge.
4. **Ask whether the space is already sprinklered throughout**, and if not, what adding that scope does to the build-out.
5. **Bring the sprinkler answer to your turf planning** so the finish class and layout get picked once, correctly.
