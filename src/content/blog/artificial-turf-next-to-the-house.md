## The code sets the slope, not a distance

The model residential code doesn't say how far turf has to stay from a wall. It says what the ground next to the wall has to do, and turf sits on that ground.

Section R401.3 of the 2021 International Residential Code (IRC) says lots "shall be graded to drain surface water away from foundation walls." The grade has to fall at least 6 inches within the first 10 feet ([IRC R401.3](https://up.codes/viewer/colorado/irc-2021/chapter/4/foundations)).

Two follow-ups sit in the same section:

- **Where a lot line, wall or slope blocks that fall,** drains or swales have to carry the water away instead.
- **Hard surfaces within 10 feet of the foundation,** such as a paver path, must slope at least 2 percent away from the building.

So turf can run toward the house, as long as the base under it keeps that fall. Which code edition applies depends on your address, so ask your building department.

## Keep the foundation showing above the turf

Turf adds height. Thornton's rules, for example, call for a sub-base at least three inches deep of compacted aggregate, with the turf on top ([Thornton Water](https://www.thorntonwater.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/2026-residential_artificial_turf_info__rules-1.pdf)). Stack those layers on the old soil by the wall, and the ground against your house comes up.

Two code sections are worth measuring against:

- **Foundation height.** Concrete and masonry foundation walls must extend at least 6 inches above the finished grade beside them, or 4 inches where masonry veneer is used (IRC R404.1.6).
- **Wood siding.** Wood siding, sheathing and wall framing with less than 6 inches of clearance from the ground, or less than 2 inches above a patio slab or step, must be naturally durable or preservative-treated wood ([IRC R317.1](https://up.codes/viewer/colorado/irc-2021/chapter/3/building-planning)).

The code doesn't mention turf, so ask your building department how it measures finished grade. A layout that keeps those clearances usually means digging the base in, not stacking it on top.

## On swelling clay, give the wall a gravel strip

All major population centers on Colorado's Eastern Slope sit partly on potentially swelling soils, according to the Colorado Geological Survey. Its [expansive soil page](https://coloradogeologicalsurvey.org/hazards/expansive-soil-rock/) names a common landscaping mistake: planting next to the foundation "so irrigation water enters the soil."

Turf is built to let water through. Thornton's rules require backing that drains at least two inches an hour. In the middle of the yard, that's the point. Against a wall on swelling clay, it sends rain and snowmelt into the strip of soil you most want to keep dry.

The Survey's homeowner guide, Special Publication 14 (1981, revised 1987), gives the alternative. It says gravel edging over fabric can "prevent percolation of water into the soils next to the foundation" ([CGS SP-14](https://coloradogeologicalsurvey.org/wp-content/uploads/woocommerce_uploads/SP-14.pdf)). The details:

- **At least 5 feet wide,** measured out from the house.
- **Non-woven geotextile fabric** underneath, lapped onto the foundation wall and glued in place.
- **No plastic sheeting.** The guide discourages it because it traps moisture and stops normal evaporation.
- **A path across it.** Walking on the rock damages the fabric, and worn-out fabric lets weeds and water back in.

The same guide draws two more 5-foot lines. Sprinklers shouldn't spray closer than 5 feet from the foundation. Flowers and shrubs stay at least 5 feet away unless they need very little water and are watered by hand.

One of its illustrations is a central lawn accented by gravel edging. Swap the lawn for turf and you have the layout. Where wildfire is a risk, that first 5 feet has a second reason to be gravel or pavers, covered in [can artificial turf catch fire?](/blog/can-artificial-turf-catch-fire/)

## Slope more than the minimum, and check it again

On swelling soil, the Survey asks for more fall than the code. SP-14 sets a 5 percent minimum for planted areas and recommends 10 percent within the first 10 feet of a foundation. That's 12 inches of drop. It doesn't recommend slopes steeper than 15 percent.

The ground by a newer house also moves. Soil backfilled around a foundation settles during the first months or years. SP-14 says that settlement is often enough to flatten or reverse the slope next to the house. So check the fall before the base goes in, not after.

## Send the downspouts past the turf edge

A downspout that empties onto turf by the wall undoes the strip. Where expansive soils are known to exist, the code requires roof water to discharge at least 5 feet from foundation walls, or into an approved drainage system ([IRC R801.3](https://up.codes/viewer/colorado/irc-2021/chapter/8/roof-ceiling-construction)). SP-14 sets the same 5 feet and says you'll usually need a downspout extension to get there.

Watch the edges too. SP-14 counts patios, fences and other obstructions "that dam and pond water" among the ways a lot's drainage gets changed. Any edging along the turf should let water cross it, away from the house.

## Thornton writes the buffer into its rules

Thornton sets a distance. Its artificial turf rules, dated 1 January 2026, require a Minor Development Permit before turf goes in. They also set minimum buffers from the edge of the turf:

- **Buildings.** 8 feet from the front wall of any building, and 3 feet from other building walls or impervious surfaces, for turf outside the rear or an enclosed side yard.
- **Property lines.** 3 feet. Turf may reach the front property line if a 3-foot buffer separates it from the street sidewalk.
- **Trees.** 3 feet from a deciduous trunk, and no closer than an evergreen's dripline.

The buffer has to be permeable: wood mulch, gravel, rock no more than two inches across, or another approved permeable material. The rules point back to the city's landscape code, so confirm with Thornton's planning department before you set the line. There's more on the city on our [Thornton page](/denver-metro/thornton-co/).

Other towns and HOAs can set their own limits. Check yours before the layout is final.

## Before you draw the turf line

1. **Measure the fall.** Drive a stake at the wall and another 10 feet out, and run a level string between them. The code wants at least 6 inches of drop. On swelling clay, the Survey recommends 12.
2. **Measure what's showing.** Note how much foundation shows above the soil, and the gap under any wood siding.
3. **Set the base depth** so at least 6 inches of foundation still shows above the finished turf, or 4 with masonry veneer.
4. **On clay, mark a gravel strip** at least 5 feet wide, over non-woven fabric, not plastic.
5. **Move the water.** Sprinklers stop 5 feet short of the wall. Downspouts discharge at least 5 feet out.
6. **Check local buffers and permits,** in Thornton and wherever your town or HOA sets one.
