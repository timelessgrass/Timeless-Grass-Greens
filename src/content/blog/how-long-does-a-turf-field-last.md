## Treat the age range as a planning reference

Use the installation date and expected use to begin a renewal budget, then update it with actual condition and performance records. A heavily used field and a lightly used one may need different maintenance and renewal decisions. A warranty period is a contract term, not a substitute for those checks.

Track worn areas, seams, infill condition and drainage after weather events. Keep maintenance records, the original product specification and any test results together so changes can be compared over time.

## Use the tests required for the field

The relevant sport, operator, jurisdiction and surface system determine which assessments apply. Surface-hardness testing is one example; other checks may also matter. Ask a qualified assessor to identify the applicable methods, acceptance criteria and testing schedule.

Do not use a threshold copied from another city’s program as your entire maintenance policy. If a result or inspection raises a concern, follow the operator’s response procedure and obtain the appropriate assessment before deciding whether care, repair or replacement is needed.

## Plan the work around the full system

A renewal scope should identify removal, infill handling, base and drainage assessment, the new surface and the documentation required at handover. Decide what existing materials can remain only after their condition and suitability are evaluated.

Ask about site access and closure needs early enough to consider the playing calendar. Obtain a project schedule from the actual proposal rather than assuming a generic number of working days. Confirm the destination for removed materials if reuse or recycling is claimed.

## Field-manager checklist

- Keep product, installation and maintenance records.
- Track wear and drainage in the same locations over time.
- Establish the applicable inspection and testing program.
- Budget for renewal and disposal.
- Assess retained layers before specifying replacement turf.
- Agree access, closure and handover requirements in the scope.
