## Keep the surface ready to putt

Remove leaves, twigs and other debris before practice. Use tools and settings compatible with the turf, taking care not to move infill off the green. Pay attention around cups and where the fringe meets the putting surface.

For a spill or sticky mark, follow the product’s cleaning guidance rather than choosing a solvent or abrasive tool. Record the material used in the green so any later cleaning or maintenance work can be matched to it.

## When the roll changes

Try the same putts in the same directions after clearing debris. Note whether the change is local or affects the whole surface. Check for displaced material, visible damage or a raised seam, and photograph anything unusual.

Brushing and infill are part of how a green is maintained, but do not assume more infill will correct every change. Ask for the specified material, amount and method before adding it. A sudden dip or seam issue may need assessment before routine grooming continues.

The [green-speed article](/blog/how-fast-should-a-putting-green-roll/) helps distinguish a useful practice target from a speed number chosen in isolation.

## Match care to use and weather

Discuss the busiest putting lines, chipping areas and footpaths at handover. The routine should reflect those uses and the product’s instructions rather than a generic monthly schedule.

Turf can become hot in direct sun, so check conditions and choose a cooler time or area when needed. In winter, follow the product’s snow guidance and avoid scraping the green to clear it. After storms or thawing, check the edges and note any displaced infill or standing water.

## Keep these details together

- Turf and infill names, plus the care instructions.
- Approved brush and cleaning methods.
- The intended roll and a few repeatable practice lines.
- Photos of seams, edges and cups at handover.
- The contact and process for reporting a change.
