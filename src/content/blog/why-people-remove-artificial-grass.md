## Decide what is no longer working

Describe the issue in terms of use. Is the area too hot when you want to be outside? Does the pet area require more cleaning than expected? Are seams lifting, water collecting or fibers visibly worn? Include when the issue started and what care or repairs have already been tried.

Heat and ongoing cleaning are real considerations with synthetic turf. A different product alone may not resolve a problem with exposure, routine or layout. Compare the space you need now with the original design, including whether a smaller turf area or a planted alternative would suit it better.

## Assess the system before choosing the scope

Recurring odor calls for a review of cleaning, infill and drainage. Persistent puddles need an assessment of the water route and installed layers. Worn or lifting areas need a closer look at their condition. Avoid assuming every symptom points to the base or that every base is reusable.

Ask for a written explanation of the proposed work: what can remain, what needs removal and why. The [installation-problems article](/blog/signs-of-a-bad-turf-install/) provides a useful recordkeeping checklist.

## Include removal and the next surface

A replacement estimate should address lifting and disposal, access, any base work, the selected surface and its ongoing care. Ask for the actual destination if recycling or reuse is offered. Available options depend on the removed materials and their condition.

Check requirements for the new proposal instead of assuming the original installation’s arrangements carry over. Include changed drainage, structures or a different property use in that inquiry.

## Compare the options

- What problem or changed use is driving the decision?
- What has an assessment established?
- Could care or a targeted repair address it?
- What would a smaller or different layout achieve?
- What removal, disposal and preparation does each price include?
- What care will the next surface require?
