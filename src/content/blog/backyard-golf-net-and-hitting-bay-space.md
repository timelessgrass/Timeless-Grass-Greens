## Size the space to the net, not the other way around

A golf net or hitting bay only works if the ball stays inside it. That starts with a decision, not a purchase: pick the net and mat first, get its actual footprint and clearance numbers, and let those numbers decide where it fits in the yard — not a guess at how much room "feels" right.

Nets vary enough by model that a single rule of thumb undersells some yards and oversells others. As a starting range, a setup guide published by one net manufacturer puts roughly 5 to 10 feet between the mat and the net for a full swing, and about 14 feet of total room length once you add space behind the net and space behind the golfer. Its widest full-size frame runs about 10 feet across, with roughly 12 feet of room width recommended around it. Smaller chipping and short-game nets need less. Whichever net you're considering, its own spec sheet is the number to plan around, because bracing, side panels and net depth all change the real footprint.

## Overhead clearance matters as much as depth

A driver swing travels higher than most people expect, and the ball can climb steeply off a mishit. The same manufacturer guide puts 9 to 10 feet of ceiling height as the comfort zone for a full driver swing, with anything under 8 feet workable mainly for wedges and shorter irons. Its recommendation is a buffer of 6 to 9 inches between the highest point of the swing and the lowest obstruction above it.

That guidance is written with an indoor ceiling in mind, but the same buffer belongs outdoors too. A patio cover, a gutter line, a second-story eave or a low tree limb can end a full swing just as fast as a garage ceiling. Walk the space and look up before the layout is set, not after the net is staked in.

## Point the line of fire somewhere safe

Every net has a front and a back, and a mishit can clear either side of it. Before you settle on a spot, trace where a ball would go if it missed the net entirely:

- **Toward the house.** Windows, siding and screens are the most common casualties of a backyard net setup.
- **Toward a neighbor's yard or the street.** A fence helps, but a net angled toward a property line puts more shots near it.
- **Toward a pool or pool cage.** Screen enclosures don't stop a golf ball.
- **Into open lawn.** The safest direction, and usually the one with the most room to work with.

Leave real distance behind the net, too — not just beside it — for the ball to lose speed before it reaches whatever is back there.

## What one Jacksonville-area community requires

Sports netting isn't only a layout question in some communities; it's a written one. The St. Johns Golf & Country Club's own [ARC guidelines](https://stjohnsgcc.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/AdoptedARB-Guidelines-2010.pdf) group "sports netting" with other portable play equipment — a category that also names swimming pools, plastic swing sets, sliding boards, skateboard ramps and plastic basketball hoops. The rule for all of it: removed from yards and driveways, and stored inside when not in use.

That guideline is dated to a 2010 revision. Associations update their documents, so confirm the current text with your own community before you plan a layout around it — don't rely on an older copy, including this one. If you're near [World Golf Village](/northeast-florida/world-golf-village-fl/) or [Glen Kernan Golf & Country Club](/northeast-florida/glen-kernan-golf-country-club-fl/), check with your own association directly; each sets its own rules, and they don't share one policy just because they're both golf communities.

## Questions to settle before the layout is final

1. **What footprint does your specific net and mat need?** Length, width and overhead clearance all come from the model's own spec sheet, not a general estimate.
2. **Where does a mishit go?** Trace the line past the net, not just the space in front of it.
3. **Does your community regulate sports netting or portable play equipment?** Get the current guidelines in writing from your association before you plan around an older copy.
4. **Is a putting green going in at the same time?** Decide the shots you want from each feature before you decide how close together they sit.
5. **What's under the surface?** Irrigation heads, drainage lines and tree roots can all move where the base should go.
