## Plan for the time you will use the space

A lawn that looks comfortable in the morning may have very different conditions later. Before choosing the footprint, watch where the sun falls during the hours you expect to use it. Give particular attention to places where children sit or crawl and where a dog spends time.

Turf in direct sun can become much hotter than living grass. Conditions change throughout the day, so check the surface when the space will actually be used.

Include shade and an alternative area in the plan. Consider the route across the yard as well as the main play space. Footwear does not remove the need to assess conditions, and pets still contact the surface directly.

## Treat cooling claims carefully

Ask what evidence supports a product advertised as cooler: which complete system was tested, what it was compared with and under what conditions. A lower reading than another turf is not the same as matching grass or being suitable for unrestricted use in sun.

Water can lower the surface temperature temporarily, but cooling may not last. Plan an exposed play area around shade and suitable conditions rather than a brief rinse. Follow water-use restrictions and product instructions, and reassess conditions before use.

## Keep a practical hot-day plan

- Map sun and shade during the intended use hours.
- Prioritize shade where people and pets contact the ground.
- Keep a cooler alternative available.
- Check conditions before use and again as the day changes.
- Pause or move the activity when the surface is too hot.
- Evaluate product claims using the actual test information.

For a putting green, discuss the hours you practice and the seating beside it. For a pet area, include a shaded resting place and a route to it. These are layout decisions to bring to a [free estimate](/estimate/), alongside the area and intended use.
