## Turf runs hotter than grass in direct sun

The clearest numbers come from [Brigham Young University](https://www.westcoastturf.com/getdoc.cfm?id=38), where researchers took surface readings on synthetic turf, natural grass, asphalt and concrete side by side from 7 a.m. to 7 p.m. Averaged across the day:

- **Synthetic turf:** 117°F, with a high of 157°F
- **Asphalt:** 110°F
- **Concrete:** 94°F
- **Natural grass:** 78°F, with a high of 88.5°F

The hottest reading in the study was 200°F, on a 98°F day. So in full sun, turf behaves more like pavement than like a lawn. That does not make it a bad choice. It means where you put it, and what you put over it, matters.

## Why it heats up

Grass stays cool for two reasons, and plastic has neither. It reflects more sunlight: about 12.6% for natural grass against 6.9% for synthetic in the studies collected by a [2024 review of 23 studies](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11272752/). And it sweats. Grass moves water out through its leaves, and the energy that takes carries heat away: 113 watts per square metre over natural grass against 58 over synthetic in the same review.

No infill and no fiber changes that basic physics. What you can change is how much sun reaches the surface, and when people and pets touch it.

## Shade does more than anything else

In the same review, 30 mm synthetic grass averaged 57.5°C (135°F) in sun and 32.2°C (90°F) in shade, with peaks of 80.1°C and 42.1°C. Sun against shade moved the surface by about 25°C, roughly 45°F. Nothing else in the research comes close.

That makes placement the first decision:

- **Play areas and dog runs go where the afternoon shade falls.** A tree line, the north side of the house, a pergola or a shade sail all count.
- **Sun-baked strips are fine for looks.** A front lawn nobody walks on barefoot at 3 p.m. can take full sun.
- **Putting greens are played in shoes.** A green in full sun is a comfortable morning and evening surface; plan shade over any seating beside it.

## Watering helps, for a few minutes

Rinsing turf does cool it, fast. In the BYU readings, watering dropped a synthetic surface from 174°F to 85°F. Then it climbed back: 120°F after five minutes and 164°F after twenty.

[Penn State researchers](https://plantscience.psu.edu/research/centers/ssrc/documents/temperature-irrigation.pdf) tried to make the effect last. Mixing 20% calcined clay into the infill to hold moisture "did not reduce surface temperature", and pre-dawn watering had little effect by afternoon. A quick rinse before the kids go out on a hot day works. A sprinkler schedule does not.

## Cooling products and infill

Infill does change the numbers, a little. In two studies the 2024 review collected, crumb rubber ran hotter than TPE rubber: 53.5°C against 45.6°C in one, and 61.2°C against 58.0°C in the other. A sand and organic infill plot measured 48.1°C.

Products sold as "cooling" turf narrow the gap without closing it. Measured against natural grass, one proprietary cooling turf still averaged 33.7°C hotter, and a cool-fiber product 12.5°C hotter.

If you are in Florida, part of this choice is made for you: the state's standard for home lawns allows only natural infill such as clean silica sand, rock or shell, with rubber limited to the ground under playground equipment. Our [Florida infill guide](/guides/florida-turf-infill-rules/) covers the rule.

## It is a surface problem, not an air problem

The worry people usually have is that a turf yard makes the whole back yard hotter. The research says it barely does. The 2024 review found the air over synthetic turf only 0.5 to 1.2°C warmer than over natural grass, and no significant difference in wet bulb globe temperature, the standard measure of heat stress.

So the risk is contact: bare feet, paws, a toddler lying down, a hand on the ground. Someone standing on it in shoes is not in any more heat than on the patio next to it.

## How to plan for it

1. **Map the shade first.** Note where the shade falls at 3 p.m. in July, and put the play area or the dog run there.
2. **Add shade where there is none.** A shade sail or pergola over a play area does more than any turf product.
3. **Keep water close.** A hose bib or a bowl near the dog's area makes a quick rinse or a drink easy.
4. **Check before bare feet.** The back of your hand on the surface tells you in two seconds.
5. **Rinse before play on the hottest days.** It buys you minutes, which is often all a quick game needs.
