## Count the hours of sun first

Grass runs on light. Turf doesn't. So the choice turns on how much direct sun the spot really gets.

[Clemson Extension](https://hgic.clemson.edu/factsheet/growing-grass-in-shade/) draws the line: if an area gets less than 4 hours of sunlight a day, it is too shady for grass to grow well. With less light the grass makes less energy, so it copes worse with heat, cold, disease, drought and wear.

Florida's most common lawn grass needs more. [UF/IFAS Extension](https://ask.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/LH010) says Floratam, the most widely used St. Augustinegrass in the state, does not persist well with less than 6 hours of sunlight a day.

Before you choose anything, measure:

- **Pick a clear day** and check the spot every hour. Note when direct sun is actually on it.
- **Check again with the trees in full leaf.** A spot under leafy trees can be bright in early spring and dark by June.
- **Don't guess on a north wall or under a canopy.** Count the hours there too.

## Why grass thins out under trees

Light is only part of it. Clemson names three more problems in shade:

- **Roots compete.** Many trees and shrubs root in the same soil as the grass and take its water and nutrients.
- **Disease gets worse.** Higher humidity, less air movement and longer dew all favor it.
- **The decline is slow.** Grass in shade often loses density steadily over a period of years.

The tree matters too. Clemson says trees and shrubs with dense canopies and shallow roots normally lead to failed grass, even when the lawn is cared for properly.

## With 4 to 6 hours, a shade-tolerant grass may work

In that range, pick a grass for the shade, then care for it differently.

Clemson ranks the species:

- **St. Augustinegrass** is the most shade-tolerant of the warm-season grasses. Clemson lists recommended cultivars for coastal areas.
- **Zoysiagrass** takes light or moderate shade better than **centipedegrass**, but neither survives heavy shade.
- **Bermudagrass** has extremely poor tolerance to any amount of shade.
- **In cool-season lawns,** fine fescues handle shade better than tall fescue.

The cultivar matters too. In UF/IFAS's cultivar table, Floratam rates only "fair" for shade, while several other St. Augustinegrass cultivars rate "good" or "very good." Even those have limits: UF describes one that does well in partial shade, but not in dense shade. As Clemson puts it, all grasses prefer sunny locations.

Grass in shade also needs different care. From Clemson:

- **Mow higher,** by half an inch to an inch, and never take off more than a third of the leaf at once. Remove the clippings.
- **Feed less.** Grass in heavy shade needs one-half to two-thirds as much nitrogen as grass in full sun.
- **Water less often,** but deeply, and only when it's needed.
- **Clear fallen leaves** so they don't smother the grass.
- **Prune low branches.** Ideally the lowest limbs sit more than 6 feet above the ground.

UF agrees on the first two: raise the mowing height in shade, and give shaded grass less fertilizer than grass in sun.

## Under 4 hours: let in light, plant something else, or use turf

Below 4 hours, Clemson's measure says grass won't grow well. It suggests letting in light first, by pruning and removing trees and shrubs you don't need. Where good grass still can't be kept, it offers two options: remove ornamentals, or plant an appropriate groundcover.

Artificial turf is a third. It needs no light, no fertilizer and no mowing, and it doesn't compete with tree roots for water. How to choose between them:

- **A bed you mostly look at:** a groundcover keeps it living.
- **A space you use,** like the path to the gate, a play corner or the dog's route: [artificial turf for your yard](/services/residential-turf/) gives you a lawn surface there.
- **A yard with both:** turf in the deep-shade strip, grass where the sun reaches.

Shade also helps turf in one clear way. In one study collected by a [2024 systematic review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11272752/), synthetic grass averaged 57.5°C (135°F) in sun and just 32.2°C (90°F) in shade.

## Think about the roots before turf goes under a tree

Turf is laid over prepared ground and a compacted base, as set out in [what goes under artificial turf](/blog/what-goes-under-artificial-turf/). Under a tree, that ground is root zone.

[Colorado State University Extension](https://csfs.colostate.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/Protecting-Trees-During-Construction-Fact-Sheet-7.420.pdf) says large, woody roots grow sideways and lie mostly in the top 6 to 24 inches of soil. Its fact sheet on protecting trees during construction warns that:

- **Surface grading** removes topsoil that holds many of the small absorbing roots.
- **Lowering the grade** near a tree most commonly severs major roots.
- **Compaction** is worst near the surface, where most roots lie. It slows drainage and interferes with the air exchange roots need.
- **Damage shows late.** Decline and dieback can appear gradually over a period of years.

CSU advises involving an arborist early in the planning. If the tree matters to you, have one look at it before any digging is planned.

## What shaded turf needs from you

Turf in shade skips the mowing and feeding. It doesn't look after itself. The damp, leafy corner that thinned your grass is the corner where debris collects on turf.

The Synthetic Turf Council's [maintenance guidelines](https://cdn.ymaws.com/staging-stc.site-ym.com/resource/resmgr/guidelines/STC_Guidelines_for_Maintenan.pdf), written for sports fields, set out the routine:

- **Get leaves off.** Left on the surface, they work down into the turf, slow drainage and compact the infill. [UF/IFAS](https://ask.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/EP612) adds that fallen leaves still have to be removed from synthetic turf to prevent wear and tear.
- **Remove organic material quickly,** pet waste included, to slow algae, weeds and moss.
- **Brush it.** Brushing helps deter organic growth.
- **Watch for green.** Moss, mold or algae may appear in underused areas, particularly shaded, damp ones. Specialty treatments exist, so check with your installer before using one.

The full routine for clearing growth is in [moss and algae on artificial turf](/blog/moss-and-algae-on-artificial-turf/).

## A checklist for the shady spot

1. **Count the hours** of direct sun on a clear day, with the trees in full leaf.
2. **6 hours or more:** you clear the line UF/IFAS gives for Floratam.
3. **4 to 6 hours:** choose a shade-tolerant species and cultivar, mow higher, and feed and water less.
4. **Under 4 hours:** prune, plant a groundcover, or use turf.
5. **Under a tree:** have an arborist check the roots before any digging is planned.
6. **Turf in shade:** clear leaves often, brush regularly, and treat green growth early.
