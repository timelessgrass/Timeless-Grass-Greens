answer:
  question: Does artificial turf make sense for a vacation rental yard?
  answer: It can, if the yard is planned for what guests bring back to it, from beach sand and dogs to grills and wet feet from the pool. Turf takes mowing off the turnover list, and that matters because every coastal city we checked caps how tall grass can grow, at 12 inches in Myrtle Beach, North Myrtle Beach, St. Augustine and St. Augustine Beach and 10 inches in Jacksonville Beach, while Denver requires regular weeding and upkeep. None of the short-term rental ordinances we read sets a landscaping standard, but St. Augustine and Jacksonville Beach limit grass as guest parking, and your HOA may have rules of its own.

## A rental yard works harder than a home lawn

A yard at a weekly rental gets a new set of users with each booking, and nobody who lives there is around to notice a problem until the next turnover. The surface has to take what guests bring and be quick to reset. Plan for four things before the layout is drawn.

- **Beach sand.** It comes back on feet, towels and chairs. Put a hard landing, such as pavers or a rinse spot with a hose bib, between the beach path and the turf, so guests have somewhere to knock sand off before they reach the lawn. Florida's synthetic turf rule, which covers single-family lots of an acre or less, requires the infill to be clean sand, rock, shell or another natural material, installed so it doesn't wash off the property ([Rule 62-308.100, F.A.C.](https://flrules.org/gateway/readFile.asp?sid=0&tid=30839130&type=1&file=62-308.100.doc)).
- **Dogs.** If the listing allows pets, plan the dog area as [pet turf](/services/pet-turf/), with drainage under the spot dogs use and a hose the cleaner can reach quickly.
- **Grills.** If the rental has a grill, set it on a pad sized to the clearances in its manual and stop the turf outside that pad, as our [fire pit and grill clearance article](/blog/fire-pit-and-grill-clearance-from-artificial-turf/) explains.
- **Pool water.** Splash and wet feet land on the edge nearest the pool. A firm edge keeps infill off the deck and out of the water, and the base has to carry water away rather than hold it. Our article on [turf inside a screened pool enclosure](/blog/artificial-turf-inside-a-screened-pool-enclosure/) covers that edge.

Standing water is a code issue as well as a guest complaint. St. Augustine makes it unlawful to allow depressions or containers where water can stand and breed mosquitoes ([Sec. 19-4](https://library.municode.com/fl/st._augustine/codes/code_of_ordinances?nodeId=PTIICOOR_CH19NU_ARTIINGE_S19-4CEGRCOPR)), St. Augustine Beach has the same rule ([Sec. 14-3](https://library.municode.com/fl/st._augustine_beach/codes/code_of_ordinances?nodeId=PTIICOOR_CH14NU_S14-2WETRUNMA)), and Florida's turf rule requires an installation that prevents pooling.

## The lawn work turf takes off the turnover list

A natural lawn at a rental keeps growing in the weeks between bookings, and the local height limit applies whether or not anyone is staying.

- **Myrtle Beach** treats grass over 12 inches as rank growth. The duty to cut falls on anyone holding, leasing, occupying or controlling the lot, and each day is a separate violation ([Secs. 10-31 to 10-33](https://library.municode.com/sc/myrtle_beach/codes/code_of_ordinances?nodeId=COOR_CH10HESA_ARTIINU_DIV2RAGRYAWADEPUNUAB)).
- **North Myrtle Beach** sets an average height limit of 12 inches on improved property, and the rule names occupants, agents and anyone with care or custody of the lot, not only the titleholder ([Sec. 12-21](https://library.municode.com/sc/north_myrtle_beach/codes/code_of_ordinances?nodeId=COOR_CH12HESA_ARTIICAMALODELI_S12-21ACOVVEDEETDENU)).
- **St. Augustine** and **St. Augustine Beach** both prohibit grass of 12 inches or more ([Sec. 19-4](https://library.municode.com/fl/st._augustine/codes/code_of_ordinances?nodeId=PTIICOOR_CH19NU_ARTIINGE_S19-4CEGRCOPR); [Secs. 14-2 to 14-4](https://library.municode.com/fl/st._augustine_beach/codes/code_of_ordinances?nodeId=PTIICOOR_CH14NU_S14-2WETRUNMA)).
- **Jacksonville Beach** declares grass and weeds of 10 inches or more in a developed area a public nuisance ([Sec. 19-1](https://library.municode.com/fl/jacksonville_beach/codes/code_of_ordinances?nodeId=PTIICOOR_CH19PUNUAB_ARTIINGE_S19-1PUNUDE)).
- **Denver** requires regular weeding, pruning and upkeep of all plantings from whoever holds title, acts as agent or leases the property ([Sec. 57-41](https://library.municode.com/co/denver/codes/code_of_ordinances?nodeId=TITIIREMUCO_CH57VE_ARTIIIMALACLUNVE)).

Turf doesn't grow, so the area you convert drops out of the mowing schedule. What stays on the list is smaller: the beds around it, anything that sprouts along the edges (our [weeds article](/blog/weeds-in-artificial-grass/) explains where those come from) and debris. Under the same Florida rule, in-ground irrigation can't be used on synthetic turf, and the local government may require the heads in that area removed and the pipe capped (Rule 62-308.100(6)). Turf needs no irrigation to grow, though rinsing still uses water; see [rinsing pet turf during watering restrictions](/blog/rinsing-pet-turf-during-watering-restrictions/).

## What the rental rules say about the yard

We read the short-term rental sections for each city below. None sets a landscaping or yard standard. What they do regulate can still shape the layout.

- **Myrtle Beach.** A short-term rental is any stay of less than 90 continuous days ([Appendix A, Sec. 203](https://library.municode.com/sc/myrtle_beach/codes/code_of_ordinances?nodeId=COOR_APXAZO_ART2DE_S203DE)). Every rental registers for a business license, and short-term rentals pay accommodations tax and hospitality fees ([Sec. 11-25](https://library.municode.com/sc/myrtle_beach/codes/code_of_ordinances?nodeId=COOR_CH11LIMIBURE_ARTIIBULI_S11-25RERE)). The nuisance code also lists tents, cooking devices, tables and coolers used in impromptu, unpermitted or unmanaged outdoor gatherings at a short-term rental ([Sec. 10-22](https://library.municode.com/sc/myrtle_beach/codes/code_of_ordinances?nodeId=COOR_CH10HESA_ARTIINU_DIV1GE_S10-22NU)).
- **North Myrtle Beach.** The rental rules cover smoke detectors ([Sec. 11-3](https://library.municode.com/sc/north_myrtle_beach/codes/code_of_ordinances?nodeId=COOR_CH11FIPR_S11-3HOUNUSSHTERE)) and parking. At license time you report the number of on-site parking spaces, those spaces must be on your own lot, and no one may park in landscape beds in a way that blocks a sidewalk ([Secs. 21-45 to 21-49](https://library.municode.com/sc/north_myrtle_beach/codes/code_of_ordinances?nodeId=COOR_CH21TR_ARTIISTSTPA_S21-46PAST)).
- **Florida, statewide.** A local rule adopted after June 1, 2011 may not prohibit vacation rentals or regulate how long or how often they are rented ([Fla. Stat. 509.032(7)](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/509.032)). That paragraph says nothing about yards.
- **St. Augustine.** Registration includes a site plan of approved parking, and the annual inspection covers fire and life safety. Each rental provides at least one stabilized parking space per bedroom offered, and stabilized parking may not be dirt, sand or grass ([Secs. 28-145 to 28-156](https://library.municode.com/fl/st._augustine/codes/code_of_ordinances?nodeId=PTIICOOR_CH28ZO_ARTIIIZODIDIRE_DIV2VARE)). Turf isn't among the materials the code names, so keep it off any space your site plan shows as parking unless the city says otherwise.
- **St. Augustine Beach.** Guests get a copy of the city's rules on noise, parking, dune protection, turtle nesting season, littering and trash, and you file a form acknowledging the city's minimum standards ([LDR Sec. 3.09.00](https://library.municode.com/fl/st._augustine_beach/codes/code_of_ordinances?nodeId=PTIICOOR_APXALADERE_ARTIIILAUSTYDEIN_S3.09.00TRLOESWIMEDELAUSDI)).
- **Jacksonville Beach.** Grass parking is allowed only at rentals with legal nonconforming parking where paved parking isn't available, and the city's other standards, including setbacks and stormwater, still apply ([Sec. 34-731](https://library.municode.com/fl/jacksonville_beach/codes/code_of_ordinances?nodeId=PTIICOOR_CH34LADECO_ARTVIISIDEST_DIV2SUST_S34-731SHRMVARE)).
- **Denver.** A short-term rental must be your primary residence, and the guest brochure has to cover parking, noise, the trash schedule and water restrictions ([Ch. 33, Art. III](https://library.municode.com/co/denver/codes/code_of_ordinances?nodeId=TITIIREMUCO_CH33LO_ARTIIISHRMRE)). In Denver, the rental yard is your own yard.

Other towns in each market write their own rules. If yours isn't listed, read its short-term rental section before you plan.

## HOA rules are a separate layer

Your covenants can govern rentals and landscaping on their own terms, and the association's approval is separate from the city's. In Florida, an association may not stop the people who own a parcel, or their tenants, from installing artificial turf that isn't visible from the frontage, a neighboring lot, a common area or a community golf course ([Fla. Stat. 720.3045](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/720.3045)). A front yard usually is visible, so the association's rules decide there. Colorado and South Carolina work differently; see [can your HOA stop artificial turf](/blog/can-my-hoa-stop-artificial-turf/). Get the approval in writing before you block install dates on the booking calendar.

## Beachfront lots: the dune comes first

Florida's rule requires turf to sit landward of any dune system, bars it from replacing dune vegetation and requires every edge and seam to be anchored to withstand wind or flooding (Rule 62-308.100(9)). On an oceanfront lot in either state, a state coastal line may also apply; our [oceanfront guide](/guides/oceanfront-turf-cccl-and-beachfront-setback/) covers Florida's control line and South Carolina's setback line.

## Turnover cleaning on turf

Add a short turf pass to the cleaning checklist:

1. **Pick up pet waste** and rinse the spots dogs used, following the turf maker's instructions. Our [pet turf cleaning guide](/blog/how-to-clean-pet-turf/) covers cleaners and odor.
2. **Clear leaves, sand and litter** with the tools the care sheet allows.
3. **Check around the grill pad** for ash or dropped charcoal.
4. **Walk the pool edge** for infill on the deck.
5. **Look for standing water** after the rinse, and note where it sits.
6. **Check seams and edges**, especially after a storm.

Anything that repeats, such as odor in one corner or a puddle in the same spot, goes to the installer with a photo and the date.
