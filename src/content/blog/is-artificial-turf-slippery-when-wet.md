## What water alone does

The clearest test of wet turf we found comes from [Ghent University](https://media.isbweb.org/images/conferences/isb-congresses/2013/oral/sb-soccer.07.pdf), presented at the International Society of Biomechanics congress in 2013. Twelve experienced soccer players ran shuttle sprints with 180-degree turns on the same artificial grass, both dry and wet. They wore one shoe model with three different stud patterns.

- **Dry,** the three soles performed the same.
- **Wet,** two of them still did. The third, with 72 short studs, made players slower. Of the 8 real slips in the whole experiment, where a player fully lost balance, 7 happened in that shoe on the wet turf.
- **The machines missed it.** Machine tests for rotational resistance and linear friction met FIFA's requirements and did not tell the dry and wet surfaces apart.
- **Nike funded the study.**

The authors conclude that wet turf offers less available traction, and that how much that matters depends on the sole. The shoe that struggled had the shortest studs. House shoes, sneakers and bare feet have none, and none of the research we read tested them.

So water can cost some grip. The slip warnings in the turf care guides we read, though, are about what builds up on or in the turf, and about cold.

## Algae, moss and a skin of debris

The [Synthetic Turf Council's maintenance guidelines](https://cdn.ymaws.com/staging-stc.site-ym.com/resource/resmgr/guidelines/STC_Guidelines_for_Maintenan.pdf) say moss, mold or algae may appear where turf gets little use, particularly in shade and damp. They advise removing organic material, animal waste included, as soon as possible to hold that growth back.

The UK sports surface builders' [SAPCA code](https://sapca.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2018/07/SAPCA_Code_of_Practice_Construction_Maintenance_Synthetic_Turf_01.pdf) describes the worst case. When contamination builds up in the infill, it usually takes the form of "a slippery sludgy material," and the code treats that as a reason a pitch can become dangerous for players.

The hard surfaces beside the turf count too. The [Royal Horticultural Society](https://www.rhs.org.uk/biodiversity/algae-lichens-liverworts-moss-hard-surfaces) says algae and moss can make surfaces slippery and should be controlled where they could cause falls. Algae grow faster in shady spots with poor air movement, it adds, and often build up after a wet winter. Stepping stones, pavers and pool coping in the same shade as the turf need the same eye.

How to clear a patch is in our article on [moss and algae on artificial turf](/blog/moss-and-algae-on-artificial-turf/). In short: debris off, brush, and treat only with a product the turf maker approves.

## Flattened fibers and low infill

Infill does more than weigh turf down. [Penn State's Center for Sports Surface Research](https://plantscience.psu.edu/research/centers/ssrc/documents/ftfsynmaint1.pdf/@@download/file/FTFsynmaint1.pdf) says it helps keep the fibers upright and cushions the surface. When infill runs low, the center warns of faster fiber wear, fibers lying over and a harder surface. The Synthetic Turf Council adds that flattened fibers can speed up wear and reduce how the surface performs, and that regular brushing keeps them standing.

None of these sources measured whether laid-over fibers are slicker to walk on, so we won't claim it. What they do say is that the surface gets harder, and a harder surface means a harder landing if someone falls.

Penn State suggests getting the target infill depth range from the turf maker, measuring with a depth gauge, and topping up with the same size and type of material originally installed.

## Frost and ice

Frost is the Denver problem. NOAA's [1991 to 2020 climate normals](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/access/services/data/v1?dataset=normals-annualseasonal-1991-2020&stations=USW00003017,USW00093718,USW00013889&dataTypes=ANN-TMIN-AVGNDS-LSTH032,DJF-TMIN-AVGNDS-LSTH032&includeStationName=true&format=json) average about 156 nights a year at or below 32°F at Denver International Airport, about 84 of them from December through February. The North Myrtle Beach airport averages about 34 such nights, and Jacksonville about 14.

A thermometer reading above freezing doesn't rule frost out. The [National Weather Service](https://forecast.weather.gov/glossary.php?word=frost) explains that frost forms when the ground and objects on it cool below 32°F, and that it often happens while the thermometer reads in the mid-30s.

Frozen turf behaves differently. SAPCA allows play on a frozen pitch only if the foothold is adequate, discourages heavy use because the fibers are relatively brittle in the cold, and warns that shock absorption drops substantially. The Synthetic Turf Council notes that light frost or ice tends to melt quickly once the sun is out. It also warns that cold wind across a damp, freshly cleared surface can leave ice behind.

The practical rules are short. On a frosty morning, wait for the sun or use a cleared path. Don't wet the turf when a freeze is coming. Our [Colorado winter article](/blog/does-artificial-turf-hold-up-in-colorado-winters/) covers snow, ice melt and rinsing pet areas in the cold.

## How traction is actually measured

You may see traction figures in turf research. They come from sports, and they describe cleats.

- **ASTM F2333.** The [ASTM test method](https://store.astm.org/f2333-04r17.html) for traction between an athletic shoe and a sports surface covers both friction and the grip studs get by sinking in. Its scope says it is "not intended for measurement of slip resistance or traction of pedestrian footwear," and that it sets no performance or safety criteria. ASTM lists its 2017 reapproval as withdrawn in 2026, with no replacement.
- **FIFA.** The [FIFA test manual](https://digitalhub.fifa.com/m/7cfc3106515eb3e2/original/FIFA-Quality-Programme-for-Football-Turf-Test-Manual-I-Test-Methods-August-2025-edition.pdf) for football turf turns a studded test foot at least 90 degrees and records the peak torque. It tests dry and wet samples. A wet sample is soaked, left to drain for 15 minutes and tested straight away.
- **Penn State.** A [2013 comparison](https://plantscience.psu.edu/research/centers/ssrc/documents/2013tractionreport.pdf) of eight football shoes found three synthetic fields gave the same or nearly the same rotational traction as Kentucky bluegrass. The spread between shoes was nearly four times the spread between surfaces. The researchers note that no one has set safe and unsafe traction levels, because the numbers haven't been tied directly to injury. Too little grip and players may slip; too much and a planted foot can get caught as the body turns.

So if a product quotes a traction figure, it describes a cleat on a field. In a backyard, the two things you can change are what's on the turf and what's on your feet.

## Who should care most

### Older relatives

The [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/falls/data-research/facts-stats/index.html) says more than one in four adults 65 and older falls each year. About 37% of those who fall report an injury that needs treatment or limits their activity for at least a day, and older adults' falls lead to about 4.5 million emergency department visits a year. The CDC's risk factors include foot pain or poor footwear, and hazards at home such as broken or uneven steps.

For a parent or grandparent who visits or lives with you:

- A firm, clean route from the door to where they sit, on pavers or deck kept free of algae.
- Shoes with real tread, not socks or smooth slippers.
- No walking on the turf until the frost has gone.
- Flush edges and seams. Our [accessibility article](/blog/is-artificial-turf-wheelchair-accessible/) covers edges, seams and small steps.

### Kids

Kids running and turning are the closest match to the sports research. In the Ghent study the sole decided how much the wet mattered, so shoes with tread are worth it on a wet play day. SAPCA's warning about reduced shock absorption on frozen turf applies to a tumble too. For play equipment and heat, see [is artificial turf safe for kids](/blog/is-artificial-turf-safe-for-kids/).

### Dogs

We didn't find research on dogs and wet turf. For older dogs, a [Dog Aging Project](https://content.dogagingproject.org/mobility-tips-and-tricks-for-senior-dogs/) veterinarian advises discouraging running, jumping and roughhousing on slippery floors, and calls nail trims essential for a dog to use its paw pads for traction. The same thinking works outside: keep the dog's usual route clean, and pick up waste quickly so it doesn't feed algae. Our [pet turf cleaning guide](/blog/how-to-clean-pet-turf/) has the routine.

### Around a pool

A pool brings wet feet, bare feet and splash together. A turf strip that stays damp and shaded beside the water matches the conditions the Synthetic Turf Council names for algae, and the coping beside it can grow algae too. Brush that strip more often and keep the barefoot route on the deck. Our article on [turf around a pool](/blog/artificial-turf-around-a-pool/) covers the barrier, drainage and heat, and [turf at a hotel or HOA pool](/blog/artificial-turf-at-a-public-pool-deck/) covers deck rules for shared pools.

## Keeping turf grippy

1. **Clear leaves, needles and pet waste** from shady areas promptly.
2. **Brush regularly** so fibers stand up and the infill stays loose.
3. **Measure infill** against the maker's range, and top up with the same material.
4. **Treat algae or moss** only with a product the turf maker approves.
5. **Find out why a spot stays wet.** SAPCA says a compacted skin on the infill holds back drainage and encourages moss and algae.
6. **Let frost melt** before anyone walks on it, and don't wet the turf ahead of a freeze.
7. **Wear shoes with tread,** and keep the dog's nails trimmed.
8. **Clean the pavers, steps and coping** around the turf too.
