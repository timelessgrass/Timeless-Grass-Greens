## Three rule books, three court sizes

Bocce has three common court sizes, and the one you build to decides how much yard the court takes.

- **Special Olympics.** The [Special Olympics bocce rules](https://media.specialolympics.org/resources/sports-essentials/sport-rules/Sports-Essentials-Bocce-Rules-2024.pdf) set the court at 12 feet wide by 60 feet long. A foul line sits 10 feet from each backboard and a half-court line at 30 feet, both drawn from sideboard to sideboard.
- **Bocce Standards Association.** The [association's court standard](https://web.archive.org/web/20161120051905/http://boccestandardsassociation.org/court_standard.htm) sets a court 76 feet long and 10 to 13 feet wide, with a pointing foul line at 6 feet, a hitting foul line at 10 feet and a center line at half the length. It notes that courts in use run from 60 to 100 feet.
- **US Bocce Federation.** The [federation's Open Rules](https://usbf.us/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/USBF-Open-Rules_03-23-23.pdf) put official courts at 86.92 feet long by 13.12 feet wide, with one foul line recommended 13 feet from the back wall.

The federation also says unofficial courts can be smaller. The [court specification Lee Tennis wrote](https://web.archive.org/web/20150218124447/http://boccestandardsassociation.org/BocceCourtSpecification.pdf), published by the association, says courts vary from 8 to 14 feet wide and 60 to 91 feet long, and that most recreational courts are smaller than the federation recommends. For a backyard, start with the 60-by-12 Special Olympics court. It fits more lots than the longer courts, and a real rule book still describes it.

## The footprint is wider than the court

Like a [pickleball court](/blog/backyard-pickleball-court-size-and-surface/), a bocce court needs more ground than its lines. Three things sit outside the box.

**The boards.** All three rule books play off side boards and backboards, and the association calls for lumber at least 2 inches thick, backed by concrete or 4x4 posts.

**A walkway.** Under Special Olympics rules, one player from each team may walk down the outside of the court to check where the balls lie, and must stay outside the court while doing it. Plan a path along one long side.

**An outlet for water.** A court boxed in by boards holds water unless it is let out.

## Stone, grass or turf made for bocce

Special Olympics allows stone dust, dirt, clay, grass or an artificial surface, as long as nothing in the court blocks a ball's straight path. The US Bocce Federation says surfaces vary more than anything else about US courts, and lists carpet, crushed stone, dirt, oyster shells, clay and synthetic carpets.

### Crushed stone or clay

This is the court the association's documents describe. Lee Tennis's specification builds it in layers: topsoil stripped and the ground compacted, at least 3 inches of hard, angular crushed stone, at least 1 inch of finer screenings to level it, then 1 to 2 inches of Har-Tru, a crushed-stone court surfacing, watered and rolled firm. Only in climates without a winter freeze-thaw cycle does the spec allow a single 3-inch layer of screenings instead. A Denver metro yard, where the ground freezes and thaws through the winter, calls for the two-layer base (see [how turf handles Colorado winters](/blog/does-artificial-turf-hold-up-in-colorado-winters/)).

A stone court needs regular work. The spec says it is kept like a clay tennis court, brushed, watered and rolled as needed, with the surface and base irrigated by hand, sprinkler or a system underneath.

### Natural grass

Special Olympics rules allow grass. The catch is that a grass court plays only as true as the lawn under it: every low spot, clump and bare patch becomes part of the game.

### Turf made for bocce

A bocce court calls for a different turf from a lawn. Two manufacturers' spec sheets show what that looks like:

- **[SYNLawn SYNBocce](https://www.synlawn.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/SYNLawn-SYNBocce-BOCCE-USA.pdf):** nylon, a 7/8-inch pile and 53 ounces of face weight per square yard, rated to drain more than 400 inches of water an hour, made 15 feet wide. SYNLawn describes it as giving true ball roll with minimal bounce.
- **[ForeverLawn BocceGrass](https://www.foreverlawn.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/BocceSpecSheet-M1123-0326.pdf.pdf):** the Court version is tufted nylon with a 7/8-inch blade and a suggested 1 to 3 pounds of infill per square foot, set to the play you want. The Play version is knitted and takes no infill.

That infill line matters. A turf court's roll comes from the product and how it is filled and groomed, not from a rule book; the association's own standard says turf courts may need future standards work. Get the spec sheet, and roll a ball across a sample before you choose. Our guide to [nylon, polyethylene and polypropylene](/blog/polyethylene-vs-nylon-vs-polypropylene-turf/) covers the fibers.

SYNLawn's 15-foot width is wider than any court width above, so a court can be turfed without a seam down its length. See [where turf seams go](/blog/where-artificial-turf-seams-go/).

## Level first, then drainage

Bocce is a rolling game, so level is most of the job. Lee Tennis's specification tightens the tolerance at every layer: the crushed stone base within 3/8 inch over 10 feet, the leveling course within 1/4 inch, and the finished surface within 1/8 inch, measured in any direction.

The same spec lets water out through 1-inch weep holes in the curbing every 6 to 10 feet, set just above the playing surface and tied to a pipe, a French drain or a small dry well. If the yard around the court sits higher than those holes, that water has to be handled outside the boards too.

A turf court also drains through its backing. [SYNLawn describes its bocce system](https://www.synlawn.com/bocce-ball-court/) with a 1-degree pitch for drainage. Ask which way that pitch runs before the base is graded, and how the base will carry water away. Our [rain and drainage guide](/blog/does-rain-drain-through-artificial-turf/) lists the questions.

## Side boards and backboards

The walls are part of the game. The US Bocce Federation lets players use the side boards at any time, and a ball that hits the backboard without first touching another ball or the pallino is dead. So the boards have to be solid and true.

- **Height.** Special Olympics sets end walls at least 160 millimeters (6.3 inches) and side walls at least 80 millimeters (3.15 inches) high, in any rigid material. The association wants every board 6 to 12 inches above the surface.
- **Material and support.** The association calls for treated lumber at least 2 inches thick, or concrete, with wood backed by concrete or 4x4 posts no more than 4 feet apart, so a ball that strikes at more than 30 degrees banks off the same way every time.
- **Concrete needs a bumper.** Lee Tennis's spec says bare concrete scars the balls and is not acceptable. A concrete curb gets a wood, plastic or hard rubber bumper board with recessed fasteners. A hinged backboard is an option to soften rebounds.
- **Markings.** The association prefers white boards, with 2-inch vertical lines marking the foul lines and center line.

For turf courts, SYNLawn lists bender board and poured concrete curbing among its edging options.

## Lighting for evening play

The association lists lighting among a court's optional extras, provided nothing added interferes with play. Settle three things before you buy fixtures:

- **Keep it out of the court.** Special Olympics rules allow no obstruction in the court that would block a ball's path, and count a ball dead if it hits a covering over the court or its supports. Poles belong outside the boards.
- **Run the wire before the base.** Low-voltage wire is far easier to place before the turf goes down. See [lighting wire under turf](/blog/landscape-lighting-wire-under-artificial-turf/).
- **Check the rules for your lot.** [St. Johns Golf and Country Club's Architectural Guidelines](https://stjohnsgcc.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/AdoptedARB-Guidelines-2010.pdf) bar exterior lighting meant to light basketball play areas or other exterior play equipment, apart from normal landscape and architectural lighting. On a beachfront lot, [SCDNR](https://www.dnr.sc.gov/seaturtle/lights.htm) puts loggerhead nesting season at May through October and notes that county and local lighting ordinances protect the turtles. Its [April 2024 list of South Carolina ordinances](https://www.dnr.sc.gov/seaturtle/volres/ordinances.pdf) shows sea turtle lighting regulations in Georgetown County, whose listed beaches include Garden City Beach and Litchfield, and a beach lighting rule in Pawleys Island. Check which county and town your lot sits in.

## Before you approve a layout

1. **Pick the court size:** 60 by 12 feet, 76 by 10 to 13 feet, or 86.92 by 13.12 feet.
2. **Measure the whole footprint,** with boards, posts and a walkway along one long side.
3. **Choose the surface,** and for turf, get the spec sheet: fiber, pile height, face weight and infill.
4. **Ask how level the finished court will be** and where the water goes.
5. **Settle the boards:** height, material, bumpers and backboard.
6. **Check the lighting rules,** including sea turtle rules on a beachfront lot, before any wire goes in.
