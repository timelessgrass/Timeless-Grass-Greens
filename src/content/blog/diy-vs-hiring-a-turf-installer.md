## Count the work beneath the surface

The job includes more than cutting and laying turf. Establish the area, access and finished levels, then identify removal, base preparation, the water route, seams, edges and the specified infill. Read the exact product’s installation instructions before buying materials.

If an existing wet area, slope, retaining feature or complex transition is involved, identify the design work needed before choosing a DIY approach. A putting green also needs a layout and contours suited to the way you want to practice. Play equipment may require a documented surface system.

## Include the equipment and logistics

Price the materials, delivery, equipment, disposal and help needed to handle them. Check the route into the yard and where material can be stored. A small square footage does not necessarily mean easy access or a simple job.

Before digging, use the applicable 811 process and follow its current instructions. Identify any private irrigation, lighting or other lines that may need separate attention. Confirm who is responsible for each locate and any work on those systems.

## Make a hired proposal comparable

Ask an installer to identify the product and explain preparation, drainage, seams and edges in writing. Establish what is included in the price and how an unexpected condition would be handled. Check applicable credentials for the complete work using the [installer verification guide](/guides/turf-installer-licensing-by-state/).

Product instructions and property requirements apply to the proposal regardless of who does the labor. Avoid assuming that a DIY project bypasses material, grading or approval questions.

## Before deciding

- Can you describe the complete installation and water route?
- Do you have the required equipment, access and handling help?
- Are removal and disposal included in the budget?
- Is any specialist design or trade work needed?
- Have utility and property requirements been addressed?
- Does the hired quote cover the same scope?
