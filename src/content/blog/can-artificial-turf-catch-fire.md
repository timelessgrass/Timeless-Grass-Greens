## Give heat sources their own space

Show the installer any grill, fire pit, heater or outdoor cooking area before the turf boundary is drawn. Follow the equipment manufacturer’s clearances and the applicable fire requirements when planning its base and surrounding space.

Keep cigarettes, fireworks, coals and other ignition sources off the turf. If heat has damaged an area, address the source before discussing a patch. Concentrated sunlight is a separate concern covered in [window reflections and turf](/blog/can-windows-melt-artificial-turf/).

## Read the test result for what it covers

A product sheet may cite ASTM D2859, often called a pill test. It measures ignition characteristics under controlled conditions. That result does not establish how a whole landscape will perform during a wildfire or with a different source of heat.

Ask for the report for the exact product if a fire-performance claim matters to the project. Check which material and assembly were tested, the test used and what the result actually says. Keep that report with the product specification rather than relying on a sales label alone.

## In a wildfire-exposed setting

If you are following the IBHS Wildfire Prepared Home guidance, keep the zone immediately around the building noncombustible; artificial turf is among the groundcovers it directs you to remove from that zone. Confirm the applicable boundaries with the relevant fire or building authority and any insurer program you intend to meet.

Include the foundation, decks, fences and nearby vegetation when reviewing the layout. The appropriate boundary is a property-planning question; a turf product’s label does not settle it.

## Before installation

- Mark every heat source and its required clearances.
- Check applicable wildfire and local fire requirements.
- Choose the turf boundary and any separate hard surface together.
- Request the exact report behind a claimed fire rating.
- Keep product and equipment instructions available for ongoing use.

A free [installation visit](/estimate/) is a useful time to show these features and discuss the proposed layout. Obtain any required fire assessment before finalizing that plan.
