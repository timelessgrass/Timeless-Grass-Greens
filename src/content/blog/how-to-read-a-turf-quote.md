## Start with the area and product

Check that each quote covers the same space and shows where the turf stops. Include beds, paths, patios and any separate pet or putting area in the layout. Ask for the exact turf name, backing, infill and any pad or border being supplied.

If a sample or alternative product was discussed, make sure the written proposal identifies the selection. Agree how substitutions would be handled rather than leaving the final material open.

## Ask what preparation and drainage include

The proposal should describe what is removed, how the ground and base are prepared and how the finished surface meets adjoining areas. Ask for the material and proposed depth where those are part of the design, with an explanation of why they suit the site.

Identify the planned route for water and any separate drain or grading work. Show existing puddles and downspouts at the visit. A phrase such as “base included” leaves too much open when comparing two scopes.

## Make the responsibilities clear

Confirm delivery and access, waste removal, utility-locate responsibilities and treatment of private irrigation or lighting. List any permits, association submissions or specialist work and who will handle them.

The quote or contract should also address dates, payment terms and what happens if excavation reveals an unexpected condition. Record agreed changes before the additional work proceeds. Keep the product care instructions and any written warranty terms available for comparison.

## The quote checklist

- Measured area and an agreed drawing.
- Exact turf, infill, pad and border materials.
- Removal, ground preparation and base.
- Drainage and finished transitions.
- Seam and edge methods.
- Access, delivery, locates and private services.
- Disposal, cleanup and handover.
- Dates, payment terms and changes to scope.

Ask for any missing detail before comparing the totals. You may find the prices cover different work rather than different rates for the same installation.
