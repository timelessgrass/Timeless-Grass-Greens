## Describe the proposal clearly

Identify whether the area belongs to your lot or is shared property. Show the turf footprint, nearby buildings and fences, the product and any drainage changes. Include views from outside the lot where visibility may affect the decision.

Ask for the current declaration and architectural requirements and the process for submitting the change. Keep the response and any conditions with the installation scope. An informal comment from a neighbor or a past project elsewhere in the community does not settle your proposal.

## Where the state distinctions matter

Colorado has statutory protection addressing nonvegetative turf in residential backyards, with limitations that still need consideration. A front yard or HOA common area should not be assumed to have the same treatment. The [Colorado HOA guide](/guides/colorado-hoa-turf-rules/) explains what to check.

Florida’s HOA statute expressly addresses artificial turf in a provision tied to visibility from specified surrounding areas. A fence or a backyard label alone does not establish that condition. The [Florida HOA guide](/guides/florida-friendly-landscaping-and-hoas/) explains why the state’s residential turf standard and private community requirements are separate questions.

For a Grand Strand community in either Carolina, obtain the documents that govern the actual lot. Ask the association to identify the requirement behind its response rather than relying on a generic regional answer.

## Resolve open requirements before work

Association consent does not answer the public review question. Confirm any grading, drainage, permit or inspection requirements with the responsible local office. If the association’s answer appears inconsistent with applicable law, obtain qualified advice on the specific documents before acting on an assumed exemption.

## Submission checklist

- Property and ownership of the proposed area.
- Current governing and architectural documents.
- Layout, product information and relevant views.
- Any grading or drainage changes.
- Written response and the requirements it identifies.
- Separate confirmation of public project requirements.

Bring the requirements to the [free installation visit](/estimate/) so the layout and price can account for the actual conditions.
